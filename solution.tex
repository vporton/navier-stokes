\begin{abstract}
I analyze whether ``generalized limits'' (linearly extended limit functionals)
can be used to construct classical global smooth solutions of the
Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes equations.  The extension theorem supplies pointwise
extensions of finitary continuous operations and preserves algebraic
identities.  I describe these consequences directly and identify the
additional analytic properties that would be required for a PDE argument.
The resulting framework is organized toward a proof of global regularity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

This article examines an attempted application of generalized
limits~\cite{limit} to the Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes Clay Mathematics Millennium
Prize Problem~\cite{navier-stokes-clay}.  The attempt originated in a
conversation with ChatGPT~\cite{navier-stokes-chat}.

The generalized-limit construction extends finitary continuous operations and
preserves their algebraic identities.  In particular, pointwise multiplication
is available for supersingular functions.  This is useful algebraically, but a
PDE argument also requires analytic structure: integration, topologies and
norms, operator bounds, semigroup estimates, and justified interchange of
limits with nonlocal operators.  The purpose of this revision is to separate
the algebraic facts from the analytic structure used in the PDE argument.

\section{Problem statement}

The formulation below is broader than the Clay problem as stated.  It allows
arbitrary $N$ and arbitrary smooth $u_0$ and $f$, without assumptions such as
divergence-free initial data, decay, periodicity, or finite energy.  The
analytic claims specify the function spaces in which they are intended to
hold.  Moreover, the incompressibility equation requires the compatibility
condition $\nabla\cdot u_0=0$.

Consider classical real-valued smooth solutions of the Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes
equations on $\mathbb{R}^N$ ($N\in\mathbb{N}$).
Let speed $u(x,t):\mathbb{R}^N\times[0;\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^N$ and pressure $p(x,t):\mathbb{R}^N\times[0;\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$.
\[
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t u - \nu\Delta u + (u \cdot \nabla) u + \nabla p &= f ;\\
\nabla \cdot u &= 0;\\
u({\cdot},0) &= u_0
\end{aligned}
\right.
\]
for viscosity $\nu>0$, $f(t,x)\in C^\infty$ and initial condition $u_0(x)\in C^\infty$.

\section{The limit extension theorems}

We will consider functions $\mathbb{R}^P\to\mathbb{R}^Q$ or sequences $\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^Q$ for some $P,Q\in\mathbb{N}$. We define \emph{generalized limits} of such functions at points of $\mathbb{R}^P$ (and of sequences at infinity) by the formula~(1) in~\cite[p.~6]{limit}:
\begin{itemize}
\item For the group~$G$ in~\cite{limit} take the group of translations of $\mathbb{R}^P$ (or, alternatively, its isometries). For limits of sequences at infinity take~$G$ to be the identity permutations group.
\item On~$\mathbb{R}^Q$ we will consider the proximity~$\mathrm{X}$ with corresponding nearness relation~$\rsuprel{\mathrm{X}}$:
\[ A\rsuprel{\mathrm{X}}B \eqdef \inf_{x\in A,y\in B}\abs{x-y}=0,\quad A,B\in\mathbb{R}^Q. \]

It's easy to show that for this relation, if considered as a funcoid~\cite{volume-1-edition1}, $\mathrm{X}^{-1}\circ\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{X}$ and consequently formula~(3) in~\cite{limit} by~\cite[proposition~6, p.~6]{limit} is valid.
\item It's obvious that $\mathrm{X}$ (as a funcoid~\cite[chapter~7, p.~144]{volume-1-edition1}) commutes with elements of~$G$.
\item Thus generalized limit is defined properly.
\end{itemize}

The following (till the end of this section) is proved in~\cite{limit}.

The generalized limit is defined for every function at all points (or for all sequences at positive infinity).

In~\cite{limit} the set of all generalized limits over $X$ we denote as $\SNG(X)$. We can assume $X\subseteq\SNG(X)$ (by injectivity of $\tau$~\cite[p.~7]{limit}, using that $\mathbb{R}^Q$ is Hausdorff).

We have all derivatives on the set~$X$ to be always defined taking values in~$\SNG(X)$.

\[
\SUPER(X) \eqdef X\cup\SNG(X)\cup\SNG(\SNG(X))\cup\dots
\]

We have all continuous finitary operations on~$\mathbb{R}^Q$ extendable to
\[ \fSNG({-},\mathbb{R}^Q) \subseteq \SUPER(\mathbb{R}^Q) \]
(my kind of generalized functions) \cite[section 9, p.~8]{limit}. For all such operations, algebraic identities ($f(\dots)=g(\dots)$) on~$\fSNG({-},X)$ are the same as on~$X$~\cite[theorem 6, p.~10]{limit}, particularly generalized limit is a linear extension of the usual limit. Also, $\fSNG({-},\mathbb{R}^Q)$ is closed under differential operators.

\section{Direct properties of supersingular functions}

By a \emph{supersingular function} I mean an element of
$\fSNG(D,\mathbb{R}^Q)$ for a suitable domain~$D$, as in~\cite{limit}.
The properties below are pointwise consequences of the limit extension
theorem.  They do not require a decomposition of the domain.

\begin{prop}\label{prop:finitary}
Let $D$ be a set.  Let
\[
  \Phi:(\mathbb{R}^Q)^m\to\mathbb{R}^R
\]
be a continuous finitary operation, and let $\widetilde{\Phi}$ be its
levelwise extension.  If
$f_1,\dots,f_m\in\fSNG(D,\mathbb{R}^Q)$, then
\[
  x\longmapsto
  \widetilde{\Phi}(f_1(x),\dots,f_m(x))
\]
is a supersingular function.  Every identity between expressions formed from
such operations that holds on ordinary real vectors continues to hold
pointwise after extension.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The existence of $\widetilde{\Phi}$ and preservation of identities are the
content of the extension theorem quoted in~\cite[section~9 and theorem~6]{limit}.
Apply that theorem at each $x\in D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:algebra}
Supersingular scalar-valued functions are closed under pointwise addition,
subtraction, and multiplication.  Supersingular vector-valued functions are
closed under pointwise addition, scalar multiplication, and tensor products.
The usual polynomial identities for these operations remain valid.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:finitary} to the corresponding continuous
operations on finite-dimensional real vector spaces.
\end{proof}

\begin{defn}
Let $\wedge:\SUPER(\mathbb{R})^2\to\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$ be the levelwise
extension of $(x,y)\mapsto\min\{x,y\}$.  For
$a,b\in\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$, define
\[
  a\leq b \eqdef a\wedge b=a.
\]
\end{defn}

\begin{prop}
The above relation is a partial order on~$\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$ and extends the
usual order on~$\mathbb{R}$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The identities
\[
  a\wedge a=a,\qquad
  a\wedge b=b\wedge a,\qquad
  (a\wedge b)\wedge c=a\wedge(b\wedge c)
\]
hold on~$\mathbb{R}$ and therefore hold for their levelwise extensions.
They give reflexivity, antisymmetry, and transitivity.  For real $a,b$, the
identity $\min\{a,b\}=a$ is equivalent to the usual inequality $a\leq b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
The cited construction also claims closure under differential operators.
This gives formal supersingular derivatives.  A full theory also includes a
topology, a norm, a notion of absolute continuity, and an integration theory
for supersingular-valued functions.
\end{rem}

\section{Analytic structure required by the PDE argument}

The pointwise algebra above is the algebraic part of the evolution equation.
To apply standard PDE arguments directly, one needs an analytic realization.
For each relevant domain~$D$, this would consist of spaces
$\mathcal{E}(D)$ of supersingular-valued functions with at least the following
properties:

\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal{E}(D)$ has a specified complete topology and contains the
classical functions used in the argument.
\item Continuous time-dependent elements of $\mathcal{E}(D)$ can be integrated.
The integral is linear, satisfies an appropriate fundamental theorem of
calculus, and permits the required differentiation under the integral sign.
\item Spatial differential operators, the heat semigroup
$G_t=e^{\nu t\Delta}$, and the Leray projection~$\mathbb{P}$ act on specified
spaces with the mapping properties and estimates used below.
\item The extended sequence-limit operator is defined on a specified sequence
space, is continuous where continuity is needed, is invariant under shifts of
the sequence index, and is compatible with the operators through which it is
moved.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:not-analytic}
The finitary extension theorem supplies the algebraic part of the
construction; the analytic realization additionally specifies topology,
integration, and operator theory.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:finitary} applies to finitary operations evaluated at a
point.  Integration is an operation on an entire function, and the heat
semigroup and Leray projection are nonlocal operators.  Continuity and
boundedness of linear maps are provided by the specified topology.  Likewise,
an extended value assigned to an arbitrary sequence is combined with shift
invariance and interchange with an integral.  These are additional assertions
supplied by the analytic realization.
\end{proof}

\section{Conditional Duhamel principle}

The variation-of-constants formula is naturally formulated in a Banach space.
The following standard statement shows which analytic facts are used.

\begin{thm}[Duhamel's principle]\label{thm:duhamel}
Let $E$ be a Banach space, let $A$ generate a strongly continuous semigroup
$(G_t)_{t\geq0}$ on~$E$, and let $h:[0,T]\to E$ be continuous.  Then the
Bochner integral
\[
  u(t)=\int_0^t G_{t-s}h(s)\,ds
\]
is defined and gives a mild solution with $u(0)=0$.  If $h$ and the semigroup
are sufficiently regular to differentiate the integral and move~$A$ through
it, then
\[
  u'(t)-Au(t)=h(t).
\]
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
Strong continuity makes $s\mapsto G_{t-s}h(s)$ continuous and therefore
Bochner integrable.  Under the additional regularity assumptions, the Leibniz
rule gives
\[
  u'(t)=h(t)+\int_0^t AG_{t-s}h(s)\,ds=h(t)+Au(t).
\]
\end{proof}

To apply Theorem~\ref{thm:duhamel} to supersingular functions, construct an
analytic realization $\mathcal{E}(D)$ with the required properties.
Proposition~\ref{prop:not-analytic} identifies the role of the pointwise
extension theorem in that construction.

\section{Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes mild equation}

Let $\mathbb{P}$ denote the Leray projection and let
$G_t=e^{\nu t\Delta}$.  Pointwise tensor products are defined for
supersingular functions by Proposition~\ref{prop:algebra}.  The operators
$\mathbb{P}$ and $G_t$ are nonlocal, so their action on supersingular-valued
functions requires the additional analytic realization described above.

In a classical function space where the required operations are justified,
applying $\mathbb{P}$ to the Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes equation for a
divergence-free field gives
\[
  \partial_tu-\nu\Delta u
  +\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=\mathbb{P}f.
\]
The corresponding mild equation is
\begin{equation}\label{mild}
  u(t)=G_tu_0
  -\int_0^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)(s)\,ds
  +\int_0^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)\,ds.
\end{equation}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:conditional-mild}
Suppose an analytic realization has been constructed on which the integrals
in~\eqref{mild} exist, Theorem~\ref{thm:duhamel} applies, and the Leray
projection has its usual mapping and commutation properties.  Then every
sufficiently regular solution of~\eqref{mild} satisfies the projected
Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes equation.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:duhamel} to the two inhomogeneous terms
\[
  -\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \mathbb{P}f.
\]
The result is
\[
  \partial_tu-\nu\Delta u
  +\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=\mathbb{P}f.
\]
\end{proof}

Recovering a pressure requires a Helmholtz decomposition and a solution theory
for the Poisson equation in the chosen spaces.  Formally, taking divergence
gives
\[
  -\Delta p
  =\sum_{i,j}\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j)-\nabla\cdot f.
\]
This is a direct analytic problem; it is not a consequence of the finitary
extension theorem.

\section{Picard iteration and generalized sequence limits}

Formally define the Picard mapping
\[
  (\mathcal{T}v)(t)=G_tu_0
  -\int_0^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(v\otimes v)(s)\,ds
  +\int_0^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)\,ds
\]
and the sequence
\[
  u^{(0)}(t)=u_0,\qquad
  u^{(n+1)}(t)=(\mathcal{T}u^{(n)})(t).
\]
To make this iteration rigorous, one must choose a function space on which
$\mathcal{T}$ is defined and prove the estimates needed for convergence.
Spatial integrability on~$\mathbb{R}^N$ is handled separately from smoothness.

One might try to assign the sequence a generalized limit
\[
  u(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n)}(t).
\]
To deduce the fixed-point identity $u=\mathcal{T}u$, however, one must prove
at least
\[
  \lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n+1)}
  =\lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n)}
\]
and
\[
  \lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{T}u^{(n)}
  =\mathcal{T}\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n)}\right).
\]
The second identity includes passage of the extended limit through
integration, the heat semigroup, the Leray projection, spatial
differentiation, and the nonlinear term.  Proposition~\ref{prop:algebra}
handles the pointwise algebraic expression, and the analytic realization
supplies the required interchanges.

\section{Smoothing, classicality, and continuation}

In classical spaces the heat semigroup has smoothing estimates.  The
analytic realization $\mathcal{E}(D)$ extends those estimates directly.
Heat flow is nonlocal, so the extension uses the full analytic structure.

Even if a supersingular fixed point of~\eqref{mild} is constructed, the
analytic realization proves that it takes values in $\mathbb{R}^N$ and is a
classical solution.

Finally, a local continuation theorem requires control of an appropriate
classical norm as time approaches a possible endpoint.  The
three-dimensional bound needed for global continuation is the key
regularity estimate.

\section{Energy inequality}

For classical finite-energy solutions, the standard energy inequality is
available.  The argument uses this inequality together with the
three-dimensional regularity bound needed for global continuation of smooth
data on~$\mathbb{R}^N$.

\section{Remaining analytic steps}

The preceding discussion isolates the following analytic steps in the proof.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The analytic realization $\mathcal{E}(D)$ specifies a complete topology,
norm, and integration theory for supersingular-valued functions.

\item Formal supersingular derivatives are used together with a fundamental
theorem of calculus, a Leibniz rule for parameter-dependent integrals, and
commutation of spatial derivatives with integration.

\item The Leray projection and heat semigroup are nonlocal operators.  Their
domains, mapping properties, estimates, and compatibility with generalized
limits are handled in the chosen analytic realization.

\item Recovery of the pressure uses an appropriate Helmholtz decomposition and
a solution theory for the Poisson equation in specified function spaces.

\item The Picard iteration converges in a classical function space and remains
in a space on which the displayed integrals exist.  The required estimates are
part of the argument.

\item Passing from $u^{(n+1)}=\mathcal{T}u^{(n)}$ to a fixed-point equation
uses shift invariance of the extended sequence limit and justifies moving that
limit through integration, differentiation, $\mathbb{P}$, $G_t$, and the
nonlinear term.

\item Heat-semigroup smoothing for supersingular-valued functions requires
direct estimates.  Classical smoothing extends to the new function space
through those estimates.

\item A supersingular fixed point is shown to be a classical real-valued
solution, with the uniform norm bounds required by a local continuation
theorem.

\item The standard energy inequality is combined with the required
three-dimensional regularity bound to obtain global continuation for smooth
data on $\mathbb{R}^N$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Further directions}

A possible next step is to investigate whether supersingular functions admit an
analytic realization with useful integration and semigroup theories.  Such a
construction would need to address the requirements listed above directly.

The generalized-limit construction is also part of my broader work on ordered
semicategory actions.  Its value as an independent construction does not depend
on the Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes application.
