\begin{abstract}
I analyze whether ``generalized limits'' (linearly extended limit functionals)
can be used to construct classical global smooth solutions of the
Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes equations.  The extension theorem supplies pointwise
extensions of finitary continuous operations and preserves algebraic
identities.  I describe these consequences directly and identify the
additional analytic properties that would be required for a PDE argument.  I
define a partial $\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$-valued sup norm and formulate the
remaining analytic requirements in terms of this norm.  The embedded
classical Sobolev realization is complete and recovers standard local theory;
a genuinely supersingular realization and the three-dimensional global bound
remain open.\fxwarning{Open analytic gap: the supersingular realization and the global bound are not proved here.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

This article examines an attempted application of generalized
limits~\cite{limit} to the Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes Clay Mathematics Millennium
Prize Problem~\cite{navier-stokes-clay}.  The attempt originated in a
conversation with ChatGPT~\cite{navier-stokes-chat}.

The generalized-limit construction extends finitary continuous operations and
preserves their algebraic identities.  In particular, pointwise multiplication
is available for supersingular functions.  This is useful algebraically, but a
PDE argument also requires analytic structure: integration, topologies and
norms, operator bounds, semigroup estimates, and justified interchange of
limits with nonlocal operators.  The purpose of this revision is to separate
the algebraic facts from the analytic structure used in the PDE argument.
Below I introduce a partial ring-valued sup norm as a candidate analytic
structure and state the additional properties it would need.\fxwarning{The extension theorem gives algebraic identities, not the analytic estimates needed below.}

\section{Problem statement}

The formulation below is broader than the Clay problem as stated.  It allows
arbitrary $N$ and arbitrary smooth $u_0$ and $f$, without assumptions such as
divergence-free initial data, decay, periodicity, or finite energy.  The
analytic claims specify the function spaces in which they are intended to
hold.  Moreover, the incompressibility equation requires the compatibility
condition $\nabla\cdot u_0=0$.\fxwarning{This setup is broader than the Clay problem and still needs the usual compatibility hypotheses on the data.}

Consider classical real-valued smooth solutions of the Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes
equations on $\mathbb{R}^N$ ($N\in\mathbb{N}$).
Let speed $u(x,t):\mathbb{R}^N\times[0;\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^N$ and pressure $p(x,t):\mathbb{R}^N\times[0;\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$.
\[
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t u - \nu\Delta u + (u \cdot \nabla) u + \nabla p &= f ;\\
\nabla \cdot u &= 0;\\
u({\cdot},0) &= u_0
\end{aligned}
\right.
\]
for viscosity $\nu>0$, $f(t,x)\in C^\infty$ and initial condition $u_0(x)\in C^\infty$.

\section{The limit extension theorems}

We will consider functions $\mathbb{R}^P\to\mathbb{R}^Q$ or sequences $\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^Q$ for some $P,Q\in\mathbb{N}$. We define \emph{generalized limits} of such functions at points of $\mathbb{R}^P$ (and of sequences at infinity) by the formula~(1) in~\cite[p.~6]{limit}:
\begin{itemize}
\item For the group~$G$ in~\cite{limit} take the group of translations of $\mathbb{R}^P$ (or, alternatively, its isometries). For limits of sequences at infinity take~$G$ to be the identity permutations group.
\item On~$\mathbb{R}^Q$ we will consider the proximity~$\mathrm{X}$ with corresponding nearness relation~$\rsuprel{\mathrm{X}}$:
\[ A\rsuprel{\mathrm{X}}B \eqdef \inf_{x\in A,y\in B}\abs{x-y}=0,\quad A,B\in\mathbb{R}^Q. \]

It's easy to show that for this relation, if considered as a funcoid~\cite{volume-1-edition1}, $\mathrm{X}^{-1}\circ\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{X}$ and consequently formula~(3) in~\cite{limit} by~\cite[proposition~6, p.~6]{limit} is valid.
\item It's obvious that $\mathrm{X}$ (as a funcoid~\cite[chapter~7, p.~144]{volume-1-edition1}) commutes with elements of~$G$.
\item Thus generalized limit is defined properly.
\end{itemize}

The following (till the end of this section) is proved in~\cite{limit}.

The generalized limit is defined for every function at all points (or for all sequences at positive infinity).

In~\cite{limit} the set of all generalized limits over $X$ we denote as $\SNG(X)$. We can assume $X\subseteq\SNG(X)$ (by injectivity of $\tau$~\cite[p.~7]{limit}, using that $\mathbb{R}^Q$ is Hausdorff).

We have all derivatives on the set~$X$ to be always defined taking values in~$\SNG(X)$.

\[
\SUPER(X) \eqdef X\cup\SNG(X)\cup\SNG(\SNG(X))\cup\dots
\]

We have all continuous finitary operations on~$\mathbb{R}^Q$ extendable to
\[ \fSNG({-},\mathbb{R}^Q) \subseteq \SUPER(\mathbb{R}^Q) \]
(my kind of generalized functions) \cite[section 9, p.~8]{limit}. For all such operations, algebraic identities ($f(\dots)=g(\dots)$) on~$\fSNG({-},X)$ are the same as on~$X$~\cite[theorem 6, p.~10]{limit}, particularly generalized limit is a linear extension of the usual limit. Also, $\fSNG({-},\mathbb{R}^Q)$ is closed under differential operators.

\section{Direct properties of supersingular functions}

By a \emph{supersingular function} I mean an element of
$\fSNG(D,\mathbb{R}^Q)$ for a suitable domain~$D$, as in~\cite{limit}.
The properties below are pointwise consequences of the limit extension
theorem.  They do not require a decomposition of the domain.

\begin{prop}\label{prop:finitary}
Let $D$ be a set.  Let
\[
  \Phi:(\mathbb{R}^Q)^m\to\mathbb{R}^R
\]
be a continuous finitary operation, and let $\widetilde{\Phi}$ be its
levelwise extension.  If
$f_1,\dots,f_m\in\fSNG(D,\mathbb{R}^Q)$, then
\[
  x\longmapsto
  \widetilde{\Phi}(f_1(x),\dots,f_m(x))
\]
is a supersingular function.  Every identity between expressions formed from
such operations that holds on ordinary real vectors continues to hold
pointwise after extension.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The existence of $\widetilde{\Phi}$ and preservation of identities are the
content of the extension theorem quoted in~\cite[section~9 and theorem~6]{limit}.
Apply that theorem at each $x\in D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:algebra}
Supersingular scalar-valued functions are closed under pointwise addition,
subtraction, and multiplication.  Supersingular vector-valued functions are
closed under pointwise addition, scalar multiplication, and tensor products.
The usual polynomial identities for these operations remain valid.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:finitary} to the corresponding continuous
operations on finite-dimensional real vector spaces.
\end{proof}

\begin{defn}
Let $\wedge:\SUPER(\mathbb{R})^2\to\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$ be the levelwise
extension of $(x,y)\mapsto\min\{x,y\}$.  For
$a,b\in\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$, define
\[
  a\leq b \eqdef a\wedge b=a.
\]
\end{defn}

\begin{prop}
The above relation is a partial order on~$\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$ and extends the
usual order on~$\mathbb{R}$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The identities
\[
  a\wedge a=a,\qquad
  a\wedge b=b\wedge a,\qquad
  (a\wedge b)\wedge c=a\wedge(b\wedge c)
\]
hold on~$\mathbb{R}$ and therefore hold for their levelwise extensions.
They give reflexivity, antisymmetry, and transitivity.  For real $a,b$, the
identity $\min\{a,b\}=a$ is equivalent to the usual inequality $a\leq b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:ordered-ring}
The partial order on~$\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$ is compatible with the levelwise
ring operations: addition is order preserving, negation is order reversing,
and multiplication by a nonnegative element is order preserving.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Let $\vee$ be the levelwise extension of~$\max$.  The identities
\[
\begin{aligned}
  (a+c)\wedge(b+c)&=(a\wedge b)+c,\\
  (-b)\wedge(-a)&=-(a\vee b),\\
  a\vee b+a\wedge b&=a+b,\\
  \abs{c}&=c-2(c\wedge0),\\
  (a\abs{c})\wedge(b\abs{c})&=(a\wedge b)\abs{c}
\end{aligned}
\]
hold on~$\mathbb{R}$ and therefore hold after levelwise extension.  The first
identity gives translation invariance of the order.  The second and third give
reversal under negation.  If $c\geq0$, then $c\wedge0=0$, so the fourth
identity gives $\abs{c}=c$.  The last identity then shows that multiplication
by~$c$ preserves the order.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
The cited construction also claims closure under differential operators.
This gives formal supersingular derivatives.  A full theory also includes a
topology, a norm, a notion of absolute continuity, and an integration theory
for supersingular-valued functions.
\end{rem}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:funcoid-super}
Let $\mathrm{X}$ be the base funcoid on~$R$.  The construction
in~\cite[sections ``Funcoid of singularities'' and ``Funcoid of
supersingularities'']{limit} equips $\SUPER(R)$ with a funcoid, hence with a
generalized nearness structure in the sense of~\cite{limit}.  This funcoid
generates an ordinary topology on~$\SUPER(R)$: if $\mathrm{Y}$ denotes the
resulting endofuncoid on~$\SUPER(R)$, then a subset
$O\subseteq\SUPER(R)$ is open when
\[
  O\in\supfun{\mathrm{Y}}{\uparrow}\{y\}
  \quad\text{for every }y\in O.
\]
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The cited construction first extends the nearness relation from~$R$ to each
singularity level.  For subsets $y_0,y_1\subseteq\SUPER(R)$ it then declares
$y_0$ and~$y_1$ near when there exist $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$ such that
\[
  \tau^n[y_0\cap\SNG^m(R)]
  \mathrel{\rsuprel{\mathrm{X}}}
  \tau^m[y_1\cap\SNG^n(R)].
\]
Both sides belong to the same level $\SNG^{m+n}(R)$.  The cited proof checks
the pointfree funcoid axioms for this relation.  Since
$\supfun{\mathrm{Y}}{\uparrow}\{y\}$ is a filter for each~$y$, the subsets
defined by the displayed condition are closed under arbitrary unions and
finite intersections, and therefore form a topology.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}\label{rem:funcoid-not-analytic}
Proposition~\ref{prop:funcoid-super} is not an analytic realization.  A
funcoid-generated topology supplies the underlying convergence structure, but
the following construction is a candidate normed structure whose domain,
completeness, integration theory, and operator bounds still have to be
studied.
\end{rem}

\section{A partial ring-valued sup norm}
\fxwarning{Suprema in $\mathbb{R}_*$ may fail to exist, so the norm is only partial and completeness is open.}

Write
\[
  \mathbb{R}_*=\SUPER(\mathbb{R}).
\]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:ordered-ring}, it is a partially ordered ring.
For $z\in\SUPER(\mathbb{R}^Q)$, let $\abs{z}_*$ denote the levelwise extension
of the Euclidean norm on~$\mathbb{R}^Q$.  In particular, for $Q=1$ this is the
levelwise extension of the absolute value.

\begin{defn}\label{def:ring-sup-norm}
Let $D$ be a nonempty set.  A function
$v:D\to\SUPER(\mathbb{R}^Q)$ is \emph{sup-normable} when
\[
  \sup_{x\in D}\abs{v(x)}_*
\]
exists in the partially ordered ring~$\mathbb{R}_*$.  In that case define its
\emph{partial ring-valued sup norm} by
\[
  \norm{v}_{\infty,*}
  \eqdef \sup_{x\in D}\abs{v(x)}_*\in\mathbb{R}_*.
\]
The same definition applies in particular to sup-normable elements of
$\fSNG(D,\mathbb{R}^Q)$.
\end{defn}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:ring-sup-norm}
The partial ring-valued sup norm has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Constant functions and functions for which
$\{\abs{v(x)}_*:x\in D\}$ has a maximum are sup-normable.
\item If $v$ is sup-normable, then
$\norm{v}_{\infty,*}\geq0$, and
$\norm{v}_{\infty,*}=0$ if and only if $v=0$.
\item If $v$, $w$, and $v+w$ are sup-normable, then
\[
  \norm{v+w}_{\infty,*}
  \leq\norm{v}_{\infty,*}+\norm{w}_{\infty,*}.
\]
\item If $v$ is sup-normable and $c\in\mathbb{R}$, then $cv$ is
sup-normable and
\[
  \norm{cv}_{\infty,*}=\abs{c}\norm{v}_{\infty,*}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The identities and inequalities
\[
  \abs{z}_*\geq0,\qquad
  \abs{z+w}_*\leq\abs{z}_*+\abs{w}_*,\qquad
  \abs{cz}_*=\abs{c}\abs{z}_*
\]
are levelwise extensions of the corresponding Euclidean identities and
inequalities.  Inequalities are expressed using the extended meet operation
from the definition of the order.  Taking a supremum gives the first three
claims whenever the displayed suprema exist.  Multiplication by a positive
real number is an order automorphism of~$\mathbb{R}_*$, with inverse given by
multiplication by its reciprocal, which proves the last claim.  Finally,
$\abs{z}_*=0$ implies $z=0$: each coordinate has absolute value at most
$\abs{z}_*$.  For a scalar~$a$, the extended identities
$a\wedge\abs{a}=a$ and $(-a)\wedge\abs{a}=-a$ show that
$\abs{a}=0$ implies $a\leq0$ and $-a\leq0$, hence $a=0$.  Thus
$\norm{v}_{\infty,*}=0$ implies $v(x)=0$ for every~$x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}\label{rem:norm-domain}
The sup-normable functions need not form a vector space.  If the two suprema
$\norm{v}_{\infty,*}$ and $\norm{w}_{\infty,*}$ exist, the triangle inequality
gives an upper bound for $\{\abs{v(x)+w(x)}_*:x\in D\}$, but a bounded subset
of~$\mathbb{R}_*$ has not been proved to have a supremum.  This differs from
the usual functional-analytic situation: an ordinary operator norm may be
infinite or undefined for an unbounded operator, but the real-valued norm on
the ambient normed space is defined for every vector.
\end{rem}

\begin{defn}\label{def:admissible}
An \emph{admissible ring-normed space} on~$D$ is a real linear subspace
\[
  \mathcal{E}(D)\subseteq\fSNG(D,\mathbb{R}^Q)
\]
consisting of sup-normable functions.  On such a space
$\norm{{-}}_{\infty,*}$ is a total $\mathbb{R}_*$-valued norm.
\end{defn}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:finite-range-admissible}
The space
\[
  \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{fin}}(D)
  =\left\{v\in\fSNG(D,\mathbb{R}^Q):v[D]\text{ is finite}\right\}
\]
is an admissible ring-normed space.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The image of a linear combination of two finite-range functions is finite.
For $v\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{fin}}(D)$, the set
$\{\abs{v(x)}_*:x\in D\}$ is finite.  Its supremum exists by applying the
levelwise extension of~$\max$ finitely many times.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-range-admissible} proves that the definition is
not empty, including for genuinely supersingular values.  It does not solve
the analytic problem: completeness has not been proved, and spatial
differentiation, heat flow, and the Leray projection need not preserve finite
range.
\end{rem}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:funcoid-topology-e}
Let $\mathcal{E}(D)$ be admissible and suppose it is equipped with an
endofuncoid~$\mathrm{Y}_{\mathcal{E}}$, for example the restriction of the
funcoid on the relevant supersingular function space.  Then
$\mathrm{Y}_{\mathcal{E}}$ generates a topology on~$\mathcal{E}(D)$ by
declaring $O\subseteq\mathcal{E}(D)$ open when
\[
  O\in\supfun{\mathrm{Y}_{\mathcal{E}}}{\uparrow}\{v\}
  \quad\text{for every }v\in O.
\]
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The proof is the same as in Proposition~\ref{prop:funcoid-super}.  For each
$v$, the value $\supfun{\mathrm{Y}_{\mathcal{E}}}{\uparrow}\{v\}$ is a filter,
so finite intersections of neighborhoods remain neighborhoods and arbitrary
unions are immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
The funcoid-generated topology is not proved here to be Hausdorff.
Hausdorffness, compatibility with the ring-valued norm, and completeness are
additional properties of the chosen funcoid on~$\mathcal{E}(D)$, not
consequences of admissibility alone.
\end{rem}

\begin{defn}\label{def:ring-operator-bound}
Let $\mathcal{E}(D)$ and $\mathcal{F}(D)$ be admissible ring-normed spaces.
A linear operator $T:\mathcal{E}(D)\to\mathcal{F}(D)$ is
\emph{ring-bounded} if there exists $C\in\mathbb{R}_*$, $C\geq0$, such that
\[
  \norm{Tv}_{\infty,*}\leq C\norm{v}_{\infty,*}
  \quad\text{for every }v\in\mathcal{E}(D).
\]
If the set of such constants has an infimum, define
\[
  \norm{T}_{\mathrm{op},*}
  \eqdef\inf\left\{C\in\mathbb{R}_*:C\geq0,\
  \norm{Tv}_{\infty,*}\leq C\norm{v}_{\infty,*}
  \text{ for every }v\in\mathcal{E}(D)\right\}.
\]
\end{defn}

\begin{rem}
Definition~\ref{def:ring-operator-bound} does not assert that every operator
norm exists.  For the PDE argument, the useful statements are explicit
ring-valued bounds for the particular operators and bilinear maps that occur.
\end{rem}

\begin{defn}\label{def:ring-bilinear-bound}
Let $\mathcal{E}_1$, $\mathcal{E}_2$, and $\mathcal{F}$ be admissible
ring-normed spaces.  A bilinear map
$B:\mathcal{E}_1\times\mathcal{E}_2\to\mathcal{F}$ is
\emph{ring-bounded} if there exists $C\in\mathbb{R}_*$, $C\geq0$, such that
\[
  \norm{B(v,w)}_{\infty,*}
  \leq C\norm{v}_{\infty,*}\norm{w}_{\infty,*}
\]
for all $v\in\mathcal{E}_1$ and $w\in\mathcal{E}_2$.
\end{defn}

\section{Analytic structure required by the PDE argument}
\fxwarning{The following items are required assumptions, not consequences of the generalized-limit construction.}

The pointwise algebra and the partial ring-valued norm above are the starting
points for a ring-valued analytic realization.  For each relevant domain~$D$,
one needs to choose admissible spaces~$\mathcal{E}(D)$ with at least the
following properties:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The ring-valued norm is total on~$\mathcal{E}(D)$, and the topology
generated by the chosen funcoid on~$\mathcal{E}(D)$ is compatible with this
norm and complete, possibly after passing to an appropriate Hausdorff
realization.
\item Continuous time-dependent elements of $\mathcal{E}(D)$ can be integrated.
The integral is linear, satisfies an appropriate fundamental theorem of
calculus, permits the required differentiation under the integral sign, and
satisfies ring-valued norm estimates.
\item Spatial differential operators, the heat semigroup
$G_t=e^{\nu t\Delta}$, and the Leray projection~$\mathbb{P}$ act on specified
admissible spaces with the ring-valued operator bounds and smoothing estimates
used below.
\item The extended sequence-limit operator is defined on a specified sequence
space, is continuous where continuity is needed, is invariant under shifts of
the sequence index, and is compatible with the operators through which it is
moved.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:not-analytic}
The finitary extension theorem and the funcoid on~$\SUPER(R)$ supply
algebraic and generalized-nearness parts of the construction.
Definition~\ref{def:ring-sup-norm} supplies a partial ring-valued norm.  A
ring-valued analytic realization additionally selects admissible spaces and
proves completeness, integration, and operator estimates.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:finitary} applies to finitary operations evaluated at a
point.  Integration is an operation on an entire function, and the heat
semigroup and Leray projection are nonlocal operators.  Continuity and
ring-boundedness of linear maps require estimates in the chosen admissible
spaces.  Likewise, an extended value assigned to an arbitrary sequence is
combined with shift invariance and interchange with an integral.  These are
additional assertions supplied by the analytic realization, not consequences
of Proposition~\ref{prop:funcoid-super} or
Definition~\ref{def:ring-sup-norm}.
\end{proof}

\section{Classical Duhamel principle and its ring-valued analogue}
\fxwarning{Only the classical Duhamel principle is proved here; the ring-valued replacement is still missing.}

The variation-of-constants formula is naturally formulated in a Banach space.
The following standard statement shows which analytic facts need ring-valued
analogues.

\begin{thm}[Duhamel's principle]\label{thm:duhamel}
Let $E$ be a Banach space, let $A$ generate a strongly continuous semigroup
$(G_t)_{t\geq0}$ on~$E$, and let $h:[0,T]\to E$ be continuous.  Then the
Bochner integral
\[
  u(t)=\int_0^t G_{t-s}h(s)\,ds
\]
is defined and gives a mild solution with $u(0)=0$.  If $h$ and the semigroup
are sufficiently regular to differentiate the integral and move~$A$ through
it, then
\[
  u'(t)-Au(t)=h(t).
\]
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
Strong continuity makes $s\mapsto G_{t-s}h(s)$ continuous and therefore
Bochner integrable.  Under the additional regularity assumptions, the Leibniz
rule gives
\[
  u'(t)=h(t)+\int_0^t AG_{t-s}h(s)\,ds=h(t)+Au(t).
\]
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:duhamel} does not apply directly when the norm takes values
in~$\mathbb{R}_*$.  Its ring-valued replacement must construct the integral
and prove the corresponding estimates, for example
\[
  \norm{\int_0^t h(s)\,ds}_{\infty,*}
  \leq\int_0^t\norm{h(s)}_{\infty,*}\,ds
\]
whenever both sides are defined.  It must also prove the Leibniz rule and the
required commutation with spatial operators.  Proposition~\ref{prop:not-analytic}
identifies the role of the pointwise extension theorem in that construction.

\section{A complete embedded Sobolev realization}
\fxwarning{This is a classical control case, not a supersingular realization.}

There is one immediate complete control case: use classical Sobolev spaces
embedded into the supersingular hierarchy.  This supplies a consistency check
for the proposed formalism, but it does not add nonclassical solutions or
produce an admissible space for the partial ring-valued sup norm.

Let $\iota:\mathbb{R}^Q\to\SUPER(\mathbb{R}^Q)$ denote the canonical embedding
induced by~$\tau$.  For $s\geq0$, write
\[
  H^s_\sigma(\mathbb{R}^N)
  =\left\{v\in H^s(\mathbb{R}^N;\mathbb{R}^N):
  \nabla\cdot v=0\right\}.
\]
For $s>N/2$, define
\[
  \mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{cl}}_s
  =\left\{\iota\circ v:v\in H^s_\sigma(\mathbb{R}^N)\right\}.
\]
Every element has a bounded continuous representative, and its ordinary
real-valued sup norm exists.  This does not imply that its ring-valued
supremum exists: a nonclassical upper bound in~$\mathbb{R}_*$ could be smaller
than the embedded real supremum.  Instead, define the embedded Sobolev norm by
\[
  \norm{\iota\circ v}_{H^s,*}
  =\iota\left(\norm{v}_{H^s}\right).
\]

\begin{prop}\label{prop:classical-sobolev-realization}
Let $s>N/2+1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The topology transported from $H^s_\sigma(\mathbb{R}^N)$ makes
$\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{cl}}_s$ complete.  The embedded Sobolev norm takes values
in the embedded copy of~$\mathbb{R}$.  Bochner integration of continuous
time-dependent elements is defined and satisfies the usual norm inequality
and fundamental theorem of calculus.
\item The Leray projection is bounded on $H^r(\mathbb{R}^N;\mathbb{R}^N)$
for every $r\geq0$.
\item For $k\in\mathbb{N}$ and $t>0$, the heat semigroup satisfies
\[
  \norm{\nabla^kG_t v}_{H^r}
  \leq C_k(\nu t)^{-k/2}\norm{v}_{H^r}.
\]
\item Multiplication satisfies the Sobolev algebra estimate
\[
  \norm{vw}_{H^s}\leq C_s\norm{v}_{H^s}\norm{w}_{H^s}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
  \norm{\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(v\otimes w)}_{H^{s-1}}
  \leq C_s\norm{v}_{H^s}\norm{w}_{H^s}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
All of these estimates remain true after applying~$\iota$, with real
constants embedded into~$\mathbb{R}_*$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The space $H^s$ is a Hilbert space, and $H^s_\sigma$ is a closed subspace.
The first claim follows from the standard theory of Bochner integration in a
Banach space.  The Fourier symbol of the Leray projection is
\[
  I-\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{\abs{\xi}^2},
\]
which is bounded uniformly in~$\xi\ne0$, proving the second claim.  The third
claim follows by maximizing
$\abs{\xi}^ke^{-\nu t\abs{\xi}^2}$.  Since $s>N/2$, the Sobolev multiplication
theorem gives the fourth claim.  The last estimate follows by applying one
derivative and then the bounded Leray projection.  The embedded estimates are
the same inequalities viewed in~$\mathbb{R}_*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}\label{thm:classical-local}
Let $s>N/2+1$, let
$u_0\in H^s_\sigma(\mathbb{R}^N)$, and let
\[
  f\in C([0,T_0];H^s(\mathbb{R}^N;\mathbb{R}^N)).
\]
For some $T>0$, the mild equation~\eqref{mild} has a unique strong Sobolev solution
\[
  u\in C([0,T];H^s_\sigma(\mathbb{R}^N)).
\]
The solution can be continued while its $H^s$ norm remains bounded.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
This is the standard local well-posedness argument for the
Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes initial-value problem: use the heat-semigroup estimates and
the bilinear estimate from Proposition~\ref{prop:classical-sobolev-realization}
in the Picard mapping.  More precisely, the heat semigroup maps the
$H^{s-1}$-valued nonlinear term into~$H^s$ with an integrable
$(t-s)^{-1/2}$ factor.  See also the semigroup approaches
in~\cite{Fujita-Kato,Kato1984}.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}\label{rem:classical-not-global}
Theorem~\ref{thm:classical-local} closes the analytic requirements only in the
embedded classical control case.  It neither proves admissibility for the
partial ring-valued sup norm nor proves a uniform-in-time $H^s$ bound for
arbitrary three-dimensional data.  Such a bound would solve the
global-regularity part of the Clay problem.  The ring-valued sup norm does not
provide this bound.
\end{rem}

\section{Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes mild equation}
\fxwarning{All claims here are conditional on a separate analytic realization.}

Let $\mathbb{P}$ denote the Leray projection and let
$G_t=e^{\nu t\Delta}$.  Pointwise tensor products are defined for
supersingular functions by Proposition~\ref{prop:algebra}.  The operators
$\mathbb{P}$ and $G_t$ are nonlocal, so their action on supersingular-valued
functions requires ring-bounded extensions on chosen admissible spaces.

In a classical function space where the required operations are justified,
applying $\mathbb{P}$ to the Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes equation for a
divergence-free field gives
\[
  \partial_tu-\nu\Delta u
  +\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=\mathbb{P}f.
\]
The corresponding mild equation is
\begin{equation}\label{mild}
  u(t)=G_tu_0
  -\int_0^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)(s)\,ds
  +\int_0^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)\,ds.
\end{equation}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:conditional-mild}
Suppose a ring-valued analytic realization has been constructed on which the
integrals in~\eqref{mild} exist, the ring-valued analogue of
Theorem~\ref{thm:duhamel} applies, and the Leray projection has its usual
mapping and commutation properties.  Then every sufficiently regular solution
of~\eqref{mild} satisfies the projected Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes equation.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Apply the assumed ring-valued Duhamel principle to the two inhomogeneous terms
\[
  -\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \mathbb{P}f.
\]
The result is
\[
  \partial_tu-\nu\Delta u
  +\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=\mathbb{P}f.
\]
\end{proof}

Recovering a pressure requires a Helmholtz decomposition and a solution theory
for the Poisson equation in the chosen spaces.  Formally, taking divergence
gives
\[
  -\Delta p
  =\sum_{i,j}\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j)-\nabla\cdot f.
\]
This is a direct analytic problem; it is not a consequence of the finitary
extension theorem.

\section{Picard iteration and generalized sequence limits}
\fxwarning{The contraction estimate and the limit interchange are still open.}

Formally define the Picard mapping
\[
  (\mathcal{T}v)(t)=G_tu_0
  -\int_0^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(v\otimes v)(s)\,ds
  +\int_0^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)\,ds
\]
and the sequence
\[
  u^{(0)}(t)=u_0,\qquad
  u^{(n+1)}(t)=(\mathcal{T}u^{(n)})(t).
\]
To make this iteration rigorous, one must choose a function space on which
$\mathcal{T}$ is defined and prove ring-valued estimates of the form
\[
  \norm{\mathcal{T}v-\mathcal{T}w}_{\infty,*}
  \leq C\norm{v-w}_{\infty,*}
\]
on a suitable subset, with a contraction property strong enough to imply
convergence in the selected topology.  Spatial integrability on~$\mathbb{R}^N$
is handled separately from smoothness.

One might try to assign the sequence a generalized limit
\[
  u(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n)}(t).
\]
To deduce the fixed-point identity $u=\mathcal{T}u$, however, one must prove
at least
\[
  \lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n+1)}
  =\lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n)}
\]
and
\[
  \lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{T}u^{(n)}
  =\mathcal{T}\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n)}\right).
\]
The second identity includes passage of the extended limit through
integration, the heat semigroup, the Leray projection, spatial
differentiation, and the nonlinear term.  Proposition~\ref{prop:algebra}
handles the pointwise algebraic expression.  Ring-valued operator estimates
and compatibility theorems supply the required interchanges.

\section{Smoothing, classicality, and continuation}
\fxwarning{Smoothing and continuation in the supersingular setting remain unproved.}

In classical spaces the heat semigroup has smoothing estimates.  A
ring-valued realization needs corresponding estimates between chosen
admissible spaces, for example
\[
  \norm{\nabla^kG_t v}_{\infty,*}
  \leq C_k(t)\norm{v}_{\infty,*}.
\]
Heat flow is nonlocal, so these estimates do not follow from pointwise
extensions alone.

Even if a supersingular fixed point of~\eqref{mild} is constructed, the
ring-valued analytic realization must prove that it takes values in
$\mathbb{R}^N$ and is a classical solution.

Finally, a local continuation theorem requires control of an appropriate
classical norm as time approaches a possible endpoint.  The
three-dimensional bound needed for global continuation is the key
regularity estimate.

\section{Energy inequality}
\fxwarning{This only restates the classical energy estimate; the supersingular case remains open.}

For classical finite-energy solutions, the standard energy inequality is
available.  The argument uses this inequality together with the
three-dimensional regularity bound needed for global continuation of smooth
data on~$\mathbb{R}^N$.

\section{Limits of the present construction}
\fxwarning{The list below is a checklist of missing proofs, not completed results.}

The complete control case in
Proposition~\ref{prop:classical-sobolev-realization} is classical.  For a
genuinely supersingular realization, the preceding discussion isolates the
following open steps.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Choose admissible spaces~$\mathcal{E}(D)$ on which the partial
ring-valued norm is total.  Equip them with the funcoid-generated topology
and prove Hausdorffness, norm compatibility, and completeness.

\item Formal supersingular derivatives are used together with a fundamental
theorem of calculus, a Leibniz rule for parameter-dependent integrals, and
commutation of spatial derivatives with integration.  Prove ring-valued norm
estimates for these operations.

\item The Leray projection and heat semigroup are nonlocal operators.  Their
domains, mapping properties, ring-valued operator estimates, and compatibility
with generalized limits are handled in the chosen admissible spaces.  The
nonlinear term requires a ring-valued bilinear estimate as in
Definition~\ref{def:ring-bilinear-bound}.

\item Recovery of the pressure uses an appropriate Helmholtz decomposition and
a solution theory for the Poisson equation in specified function spaces.

\item The Picard iteration remains in an admissible space on which the
displayed integrals exist.  Prove a contraction or an alternative convergence
argument using ring-valued estimates and a precise meaning of convergence.

\item Passing from $u^{(n+1)}=\mathcal{T}u^{(n)}$ to a fixed-point equation
uses shift invariance of the extended sequence limit and justifies moving that
limit through integration, differentiation, $\mathbb{P}$, $G_t$, and the
nonlinear term.

\item Heat-semigroup smoothing for supersingular-valued functions requires
direct ring-valued estimates.  Classical smoothing does not automatically
extend to the new function space.

\item A supersingular fixed point is shown to be a classical real-valued
solution, with the uniform norm bounds required by a local continuation
theorem.

\item The standard energy inequality is combined with the required
three-dimensional regularity bound to obtain global continuation for smooth
data on $\mathbb{R}^N$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{rem}
The items above are not proved by the generalized-limit construction or by
the partial ring-valued norm.  In particular, this article does not prove
global existence of classical smooth solutions of the three-dimensional
Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes equations.  As recorded in the official Clay problem
description~\cite{navier-stokes-clay}, that global regularity question remains
the central unresolved issue.\fxerror{The central global-existence claim is not established in this document.}
\end{rem}
