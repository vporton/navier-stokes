\section{Introduction}

This is a rectified proof by ChatGPT~\cite{navier-stokes-chat} of the Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes Clay Math Millennium Prize Problem~\cite{navier-stokes-clay}, using the approach of generalized limits~\cite{limit} (more general generalized functions than distributions) suggested to the LLM (large  language model) by me. In fact, the ChatGPT's proof didn't settle the energy inequality, but only existence of solutions, despite it claimed~\cite{navier-stokes-chat} a full solution.

In my approach multiplication of every two functions is defined, what allows to write formulas undefined for distributions.

So, I did a part of the work (the theorem that $\lim$ (limit) functional can be linearly extended to the space of all functions and that on the space of extended limits all continuous operations can be extended with preservation of all algebraic identities). And ChatGPT did a part of work (applied known methods with addition of my this theorem).

Extraordinary claims need extraordinary evidence. The claim is a Millennium Prize Problem solution. The evidence of this ``functional extension'' theorem is an extraordinary evidence, because it is a new very powerful theorem in general topology and its proof is extraordinarily longer than a noob would expect.

I had a little trouble to understand the ChatGPT's proof, because while being an expert in general topology, I am no expert in differential equations. But I was able to write down my more detailed (and in parts more elegant than ChatGPT's) proof.

\section{Problem statement}

The problem statement is wider (``weaker'') than the Clay problem as stated.  It allows arbitrary
$N$ and arbitrary smooth $u_0$ and $f$, without assumptions such as
divergence-free initial data, decay, periodicity, or finite energy.  Smooth
functions on $\mathbb{R}^N$ need not be integrable or have finite energy. So, the Clay's statement follows from it.

We are going to prove existence (in the sense of classical real-number solutions) and smoothness~($C^\infty$) of solutions to Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes equations on $\mathbb{R}^N$ ($N\in\mathbb{N}$).
Let speed $u(x,t):\mathbb{R}^N\times[0;\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^N$ and pressure $p(x,t):\mathbb{R}^N\times[0;\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$.
\[
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t u - \nu\Delta u + (u \cdot \nabla) u + \nabla p &= f ;\\
\nabla \cdot u &= 0;\\
u({\cdot},0) &= u_0
\end{aligned}
\right.
\]
for viscosity $\nu>0$, $f(t,x)\in C^\infty$ and initial condition $u_0(x)\in C^\infty$.

\section{The limit extension theorems}

We will consider functions $\mathbb{R}^P\to\mathbb{R}^Q$ or sequences $\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^Q$ for some $P,Q\in\mathbb{N}$. We define \emph{generalized limits} of such functions at points of $\mathbb{R}^P$ (and of sequences at infinity) by the formula~(1) in~\cite[p.~6]{limit}:
\begin{itemize}
\item For the group~$G$ in~\cite{limit} take the group of translations of $\mathbb{R}^P$ (or, alternatively, its isometries). For limits of sequences at infinity take~$G$ to be the identity permutations group.
\item On~$\mathbb{R}^Q$ we will consider the proximity~$\mathrm{X}$ with corresponding nearness relation~$\rsuprel{\mathrm{X}}$:
\[ A\rsuprel{\mathrm{X}}B \eqdef \inf_{x\in A,y\in B}\abs{x-y}=0,\quad A,B\in\mathbb{R}^Q. \]

It's easy to show that for this relation, if considered as a funcoid~\cite{volume-1-edition1}, $\mathrm{X}^{-1}\circ\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{X}$ and consequently formula~(3) in~\cite{limit} by~\cite[proposition~6, p.~6]{limit} is valid.
\item It's obvious that $\mathrm{X}$ (as a funcoid~\cite[chapter~7, p.~144]{volume-1-edition1}) commutes with elements of~$G$.
\item Thus generalized limit is defined properly.
\end{itemize}

The following (till the end of this section) is proved in~\cite{limit}.

The generalized limit is defined for every function at all points (or for all sequences at positive infinity).

In~\cite{limit} the set of all generalized limits over $X$ we denote as $\SNG(X)$. We can assume $X\subseteq\SNG(X)$ (by injectivity of $\tau$~\cite[p.~7]{limit}, using that $\mathbb{R}^Q$ is Hausdorff).

We have all derivatives on the set~$X$ to be always defined taking values in~$\SNG(X)$.

\[
\SUPER(X) \eqdef X\cup\SNG(X)\cup\SNG(\SNG(X))\cup\dots
\]

We have all continuous finitary operations on~$\mathbb{R}^Q$ extendable to
\[ \fSNG({-},\mathbb{R}^Q) \subseteq \SUPER(\mathbb{R}^Q) \]
(my kind of generalized functions) \cite[section 9, p.~8]{limit}. For all such operations, algebraic identities ($f(\dots)=g(\dots)$) on~$\fSNG({-},X)$ are the same as on~$X$~\cite[theorem 6, p.~10]{limit}, particularly generalized limit is a linear extension of the usual limit. Also, $\fSNG({-},\mathbb{R}^Q)$ is closed under differential operators.

\section{Continuous supersingular functions}

Let $X$ be some Lebesgue measurable set and some element $0\in X$ (not necessarily real zero or zero vector).

\begin{defn}
Let $\wedge:\SUPER(\mathbb{R})^2\to\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$ be the
levelwise extension of the continuous operation
\[
  (x,y)\mapsto\min\{x,y\}
\]
provided by the limit extension theorem.  For $a,b\in\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$,
define
\[
  a\leq b \eqdef a\wedge b=a.
\]
\end{defn}

\begin{prop}
The above defined relation is a partial order on~$\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$ and
extends the usual order on~$\mathbb{R}$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The identities
\[
  a\wedge a=a,\qquad
  a\wedge b=b\wedge a,\qquad
  (a\wedge b)\wedge c=a\wedge(b\wedge c)
\]
hold on~$\mathbb{R}$ and therefore hold for their levelwise extensions by the
limit extension theorem.  Thus $a\leq a$.  If $a\leq b$ and $b\leq a$, then
\[
  a=a\wedge b=b\wedge a=b.
\]
If $a\leq b$ and $b\leq c$, then
\[
  a\wedge c=(a\wedge b)\wedge c
  =a\wedge(b\wedge c)=a\wedge b=a,
\]
so $a\leq c$.  Finally, for $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ the equality
$\min\{a,b\}=a$ is equivalent to the usual inequality $a\leq b$.
\end{proof}

The calculus facts used later are stated after the integral definitions.

\section{Lebesgue integrals}

By a \emph{supersingular function} or a solution in \emph{supersingularities} I mean a function $f\in\fSNG(X,\mathbb{R})$ (where~$X$ is a suitable space), see~\cite{limit}.

\begin{rem}
(Lebesgue) integrals could be instead defined on the space of supersingular
functions the same way as Lebesgue integrals on the space of functions with
real number values.  This uses suprema of sets of supersingularities only in
the cases where those suprema are known to exist.  But for our purposes it is
enough to define integrals on the space of continuous generalized limits
functions, and this special case is much simpler to prove properties of.
\end{rem}

Define Lebesgue integrals on the space of continuous generalized limits
functions in the usual way for functions valued in the ring $R$, but only in the
absolutely convergent case.  Namely, for a measurable set $A$ and a function
$f$ with values in a compatible real-complete ring-valued normed module over
$R$, we
write $\int_A f(x)\,dx$ only when the approximating simple-function sums are
absolutely convergent in the norm topology introduced below; in that case the
integral is the norm limit of those sums.  No general existence theorem is
claimed here.  This is the only case used in the subsequent sections.

It is easy to show that this definition agrees with the usual scalar Lebesgue
integral on real-valued functions and is compatible with scalar multiplication
by real numbers whenever both sides are defined.

Below, we will, contrary to the usual notation, denote~$L^1(X)$ the space of functions on~$X$ integrable by the above formula, not all Lebesgue integrable functions.

\section{Generalized ring-valued Banach and Sobolev spaces}

Let
\[
  R\eqdef\SUPER(\mathbb{R})
\]
with the order defined above.  Let $\mathrm{X}_{\SUPER}$ denote the funcoid on
$R$ obtained from the funcoid of supersingularities in~\cite[section 10,
p.~11]{limit}; explicitly, nearness on different singularity levels is
compared after applying enough copies of the embedding~$\tau$ to place both
sets in the same level.  This funcoid induces a topology on $R$: a set
$U\subseteq R$ is open iff, for every $a\in U$,
\[
  U\in\supfun{\mathrm{X}_{\SUPER}}{\uparrow}\{a\}.
\]
All occurrences of ``continuous generalized-valued function'' below mean
continuous with respect to the ordinary topology on the source and this
$\mathrm{X}_{\SUPER}$-induced topology on the target.  Equivalently, for a
funcoid $\mu$ on the source, the corresponding funcoid-continuity condition is
\[
  f\circ\mu\sqsubseteq \mathrm{X}_{\SUPER}\circ f,
\]
as in~\cite[p.~7]{limit}.

The order on $R$ allows one to define norm-like quantities whenever the
required suprema and integrals are known to exist.  Let
\[
  \abs{a}_{\SUPER}\eqdef a\vee(-a),\qquad
  a\vee b\eqdef -((-a)\wedge(-b)).
\]

\begin{defn}
A ring-valued normed module over $R$ is an $R$-module $E$ together with a
partially defined map
\[
  \norm{\cdot}_E:E\to R
\]
such that, whenever the displayed expressions are defined,
\[
  \norm{x}_E\geq0,\qquad
  \norm{x}_E=0\Longleftrightarrow x=0,\qquad
  \norm{ax}_E=\abs{a}_{\SUPER}\norm{x}_E,
\]
and
\[
  \norm{x+y}_E\leq\norm{x}_E+\norm{y}_E.
\]
It is called a generalized ring-valued seminormed module if the implication
$\norm{x}_E=0\Rightarrow x=0$ is omitted.
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}
Let $E$ be an $R$-valued normed module.  Its norm topology $\tau_E$ is generated
by the balls
\[
  B_\epsilon(x)\eqdef
  \setcond{y\in E}{\norm{y-x}_E<\epsilon},
  \qquad \epsilon\in\mathbb{R},\ \epsilon>0,
\]
where the real number $\epsilon$ is embedded into $R\subseteq\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$.
Equivalently, $\tau_E$ is the topology induced by the metric-neighborhood
funcoid whose point-neighborhood filter at $x$ is generated by these balls.
For $E=R$ with $\norm{a}_R=\abs{a}_{\SUPER}$, this norm topology is required to
be at least as strong as the $\mathrm{X}_{\SUPER}$-induced topology; in
applications we use only norms satisfying this compatibility condition.  Hence
norm convergence implies supersingular-funcoid convergence, and norm-continuous
maps are continuous for the funcoid topology.
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}
Let $E$ be an $R$-valued normed module.  A sequence $(x_n)$ is real-Cauchy if
for every real $\epsilon>0$ there is $N$ such that
\[
  \norm{x_n-x_m}_E\leq\epsilon
  \quad\text{for all }m,n\geq N.
\]
The module $E$ is called real-complete, or a generalized Banach module, if
every real-Cauchy sequence converges to an element of $E$ in the norm topology
$\tau_E$.  This definition uses real $\epsilon$'s because the classical
continuation theorem requires a genuine real topology.  One may also define a
purely $R$-Cauchy notion by allowing generalized positive $\epsilon\in R$, but
that gives a different and stronger structure and is not presently used.
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}
Let $E$ be a compatible real-complete $R$-valued normed module.  A series
$\sum_n x_n$ in $E$ is absolutely convergent if
\[
  \sum_n \norm{x_n}_E
\]
converges in the ordered ring $R$ and its partial sums are bounded above by a
real element of $R$.  An $E$-valued integral is absolutely convergent if its
approximating simple-function sums satisfy the analogous condition.  In both
cases convergence of the original series or integral means convergence in the
norm topology~$\tau_E$, and then also convergence in the
$\mathrm{X}_{\SUPER}$-funcoid topology by compatibility.
\end{defn}

\begin{thm}
Let $E$ and $F$ be compatible real-complete $R$-valued normed modules, and let
$T:E\to F$ be a continuous $R$-linear map.  Then $T$ preserves real-Cauchy
sequences and absolutely convergent series.  Moreover, whenever an integral
$\int_A f(x)\,dx$ exists in $E$, the integral $\int_A T(f(x))\,dx$ exists in
$F$ and
\[
  T\left(\int_A f(x)\,dx\right)=\int_A T(f(x))\,dx.
\]
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
Continuity gives $x_n\to x$ in $E$ implies $T(x_n)\to T(x)$ in $F$.  Hence a
real-Cauchy sequence or an absolutely convergent series in $E$ is sent to a
real-Cauchy sequence or absolutely convergent series in $F$.  The integral
statement follows by applying the same argument to the defining simple-function
approximations and using linearity of $T$.
\end{proof}

For a generalized-valued scalar or vector function $v$ define, when the
right-hand side exists,
\[
  \abs{v(x)}_{\SUPER}
  \eqdef \left(\sum_i \abs{v_i(x)}_{\SUPER}^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
For $1\leq p<\infty$ define the ring-valued $L^p$ quantity
\[
  \norm{v}_{L^p_{\SUPER}}
  \eqdef \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}\abs{v(x)}_{\SUPER}^p\,dx\right)^{1/p},
\]
and define
\[
  \norm{v}_{L^\infty_{\SUPER}}
  \eqdef \esssup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^N}\abs{v(x)}_{\SUPER}
\]
when this essential supremum exists in the ordered ring
$R$.

Define $L^p_R(\mathbb{R}^N)$ to be the set of generalized-valued measurable
functions for which $\norm{v}_{L^p_{\SUPER}}$ is defined and finite in $R$.
After quotienting by the relation $\norm{v-w}_{L^p_{\SUPER}}=0$, this is an
ring-valued seminormed module over $R$, and it is a ring-valued normed module
over $R$ if the zero-norm quotient is separated.

For an integer $m\geq0$ define the ring-valued Sobolev quantity
\[
  \norm{v}_{H^m_{\SUPER}}^2
  \eqdef \sum_{\abs{\alpha}\leq m}
  \norm{\partial^\alpha v}_{L^2_{\SUPER}}^2,
\]
whenever all derivatives, integrals, sums, and square roots are defined.

Define
\[
  H^m_R(\mathbb{R}^N)
  \eqdef
  \setcond{v\in\fSNG({-},R^N)}
  {\partial^\alpha v\in L^2_R(\mathbb{R}^N)\text{ for every }\abs{\alpha}\leq m}
\]
modulo the zero $H^m_{\SUPER}$ seminorm.  When the quotient is separated and
real-complete, $H^m_R(\mathbb{R}^N)$ is a generalized ring-valued Sobolev
Banach module.  If the inner product
\[
  \langle v,w\rangle_{H^m_R}
  \eqdef
  \sum_{\abs{\alpha}\leq m}
  \int_{\mathbb{R}^N}\partial^\alpha v\cdot\partial^\alpha w\,dx
\]
is defined, it is also a generalized Hilbert module in the formal sense that
$\norm{v}_{H^m_{\SUPER}}^2=\langle v,v\rangle_{H^m_R}$.

Similarly, operator norms can be defined by
\[
  \norm{A}_{X_{\SUPER}\to Y_{\SUPER}}
  \eqdef
  \sup_{\norm{v}_{X_{\SUPER}}\leq 1}\norm{Av}_{Y_{\SUPER}},
\]
when the displayed supremum exists.  These definitions are partial, just as
classical operator norms are meaningful only for operators acting between
specified normed spaces.

\begin{rem}
These are Banach/Sobolev structures in the generalized ring-valued sense above.
They are not automatically classical Banach spaces.  A continuation theorem for
classical Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes solutions still needs a real-valued bound, for
instance a real number $M$ such that
\[
  \norm{u(t)}_{H^m_{\SUPER}}\leq M
  \quad\text{for }0<t<T.
\]
When $u(t)$ is classical, this ring-valued Sobolev quantity agrees with the
usual $H^m$ norm, so such a real bound implies the classical bound required in
the endpoint argument.
\end{rem}

\begin{thm}
Let $E$ be a compatible real-complete $R$-valued normed module.  If an
$E$-valued integral is absolutely convergent on a measurable set, then it is
convergent.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
For series, the triangle inequality gives
\[
  \norm{\sum_{k=m}^n x_k}_E
  \leq\sum_{k=m}^n\norm{x_k}_E.
\]
Since the series of norms converges, its tails are smaller than every real
$\epsilon>0$ eventually; hence the partial sums of $\sum_n x_n$ are real-Cauchy
in $E$.  The proof for integrals is the same argument applied to the
approximating simple-function sums.  Real-completeness gives a limit in the
norm topology, and compatibility with $\mathrm{X}_{\SUPER}$ gives convergence
in the supersingular-funcoid topology as well.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Properties of Lebesgue integrals}

\begin{prop}
The ordered ring $R=\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$ is closed under real-epsilon order
limits: if $r,\alpha\in R$ and
\[
  r\leq\alpha+\epsilon
\]
for every real $\epsilon>0$, then $r\leq\alpha$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
For real numbers this is the usual order property of $\mathbb{R}$.  We lift it
through the finite hierarchy of singularities.

First suppose $r$ and $\alpha$ belong to the same singularity level.  By
\cite[pp.~6--8]{limit}, singularities inject into their $F$-singularity
representation by~$\Phi$, and continuous finitary operations on the base are
computed in that representation pointwise on the corresponding filters or
funcoids.  The operations $+$ and $\wedge=\min$ are continuous, so
\[
  r\leq\alpha+\epsilon
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  r\wedge(\alpha+\epsilon)=r
\]
is transported by~$\Phi$ to the same pointwise assertion in the previous
level of the hierarchy.  Applying the real case at level~$0$ and then
inducting over the finite singularity level gives the pointwise identity
\[
  \Phi(r\wedge\alpha)=\Phi(r).
\]
Since~$\Phi$ is injective, $r\wedge\alpha=r$, hence $r\leq\alpha$.

For arbitrary $r,\alpha\in R$, apply enough copies of the injective embedding
$\tau$ to put both elements in one common singularity level.  The construction
of operations on $\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$ in \cite[section~9]{limit} is precisely
this level-raising procedure, and $\tau$ preserves and reflects the identities
formed from the extended continuous operations $+$ and~$\wedge$.  Therefore
the same-level result applied to the raised elements reflects back to
$r\wedge\alpha=r$ in~$R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{defn}
Whenever the displayed interval integrals exist, define the oriented interval
integral by
\[
  \int_\alpha^\beta f(t)\,dt
  \eqdef
  \begin{cases}
    \int_{[\alpha,\beta]} f(t)\,dt,& \alpha\leq\beta,\\
    -\int_{[\beta,\alpha]} f(t)\,dt,& \beta<\alpha.
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{defn}

\begin{prop}
Let $E$ be a compatible real-complete ring-valued normed module over
$R=\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$.  If $x_n\to x$ in the norm topology of $E$ and
\[
  \norm{x_n}_E\leq M
\]
for every $n$, where $M\in R$, then $\norm{x}_E\leq M$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
For every real $\eta>0$, choose $n$ such that
$\norm{x-x_n}_E\leq\eta$.  Then
\[
  \norm{x}_E\leq\norm{x-x_n}_E+\norm{x_n}_E\leq M+\eta .
\]
The real-epsilon order-limit property of $R$ gives $\norm{x}_E\leq M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}[Basic interval-integral rules]
Let $E$ be a compatible real-complete ring-valued normed module over
$R=\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$.  For functions whose displayed integrals exist, the
interval integral is linear and finitely additive over adjacent real
intervals: for $c,d\in R$,
\[
  \int_\alpha^\beta (cf(t)+dg(t))\,dt
  =
  c\int_\alpha^\beta f(t)\,dt+d\int_\alpha^\beta g(t)\,dt
\]
and, for $\alpha\leq\beta\leq\gamma$,
\[
  \int_\alpha^\gamma f(t)\,dt
  =
  \int_\alpha^\beta f(t)\,dt+\int_\beta^\gamma f(t)\,dt .
\]
The same additivity holds for oriented intervals after the signs in the
definition above are applied.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Both assertions hold for simple functions and their simple-function sums.
Passing to the defining norm limits preserves the equalities because addition
and scalar multiplication are continuous in the norm topology.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}[Bounded-integral estimate]
Let $A$ be a measurable set of finite real measure, let $E$ be a compatible
real-complete ring-valued normed module over $R=\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$, and let
$f:A\to E$ be integrable.  If $M\in R$ and
\[
  \norm{f(t)}_E\leq M
  \quad\text{for all }t\in A,
\]
then
\[
  \norm{\int_A f(t)\,dt}_E\leq M\,|A|.
\]
Consequently, for real $\alpha,\beta$,
\[
  \norm{\int_\alpha^\beta f(t)\,dt}_E
  \leq M\,\abs{\beta-\alpha}
\]
whenever the oriented interval integral exists and the same pointwise bound
holds on the ordinary interval between $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
For a simple-function approximating sum
$S=\sum_i m_i y_i$ with each $m_i\geq0$, $\sum_i m_i=|A|$, and
$\norm{y_i}_E\leq M$, the triangle inequality and homogeneity with real
coefficients give
\[
  \norm{S}_E\leq\sum_i m_i\norm{y_i}_E\leq M\sum_i m_i=M\,|A|.
\]
The integral is the norm limit of such sums, so the closed norm-estimate
proposition passes this bound to the integral.  The oriented-interval estimate
is the same argument applied to the underlying non-oriented interval; the sign
does not change the norm.
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}[Fundamental theorem for ring-valued integral values]
Let $a<b$ be real numbers, and let $E$ be a compatible real-complete
ring-valued normed module over $R=\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$.

Let $f:[a,b]\to E$ be norm-continuous at $x\in(a,b)$, and assume that the
integrals below exist for all sufficiently small real $h$ with
$x+h\in[a,b]$.
If
\[
  F(y)\eqdef\int_a^y f(t)\,dt ,
\]
then $F$ is differentiable at $x$ in the norm topology and
\[
  F'(x)=f(x).
\]
Consequently the same derivative equality also holds in the
$\mathrm{X}_{\SUPER}$-funcoid topology.  In particular this applies to
$E=R$, and to $\fSNG$-valued modules whenever the stated ring-valued norm,
integrals, and norm-continuity condition are available.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
For real $h\neq0$ with $x+h\in[a,b]$,
\[
  \frac{F(x+h)-F(x)}{h}-f(x)
  =
  \frac{1}{h}\int_x^{x+h}(f(t)-f(x))\,dt .
\]
Here the equality follows from interval additivity and from
$\int_x^{x+h} f(x)\,dt=hf(x)$.
Let $\epsilon>0$ be real.  By norm-continuity of $f$ at $x$, there is
$\delta>0$ such that
\[
  \norm{f(t)-f(x)}_E\leq\epsilon
  \quad\text{whenever } t\in[a,b]\text{ and } \abs{t-x}<\delta .
\]
Fix such an $h$ with $0<\abs{h}<\delta$, and put $g(t)=f(t)-f(x)$ on the
ordinary real interval between $x$ and $x+h$.  The bounded-integral estimate
gives
\[
  \norm{\int_x^{x+h}g(t)\,dt}_E\leq\epsilon\abs{h}.
\]
Therefore
\[
  \norm{\frac{F(x+h)-F(x)}{h}-f(x)}_E
  \leq\epsilon .
\]
Since this holds for every real $\epsilon>0$ once $\abs{h}$ is sufficiently
small, the difference quotient converges to $f(x)$ in the norm topology.
Compatibility of the norm topology with $\mathrm{X}_{\SUPER}$ then gives the
same convergence in the supersingular-funcoid topology.
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}
Let $W:[a,b]\to E$ be norm-continuous and norm-differentiable on $(a,b)$.  If
$W'(t)=0$ for every $t\in(a,b)$, then $W$ is constant.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\alpha,\beta\in(a,b)$ with $\alpha<\beta$ and a real $\epsilon>0$.  Since
$W'(t)=0$, for every $t\in[\alpha,\beta]$ there is a radius $\delta(t)>0$ such
that
\[
  \norm{W(y)-W(t)}_E\leq\epsilon\abs{y-t}
\]
whenever $y\in[\alpha,\beta]$ and $\abs{y-t}<\delta(t)$.  By the Cousin
covering lemma for compact real intervals, choose a tagged partition
$\alpha=t_0<t_1<\dots<t_n=\beta$ with tags $\xi_i\in[t_{i-1},t_i]$ and
$[t_{i-1},t_i]\subset(\xi_i-\delta(\xi_i),\xi_i+\delta(\xi_i))$.  Then
\[
  \norm{W(t_i)-W(t_{i-1})}_E
  \leq
  \norm{W(t_i)-W(\xi_i)}_E+\norm{W(\xi_i)-W(t_{i-1})}_E
  \leq\epsilon(t_i-t_{i-1}).
\]
Therefore
\[
  \norm{W(\beta)-W(\alpha)}_E
  \leq\epsilon(\beta-\alpha).
\]
Since this holds for every real $\epsilon>0$, the real-epsilon order-limit
property gives $\norm{W(\beta)-W(\alpha)}_E=0$, hence
$W(\beta)=W(\alpha)$.  Norm-continuity extends the equality to endpoints.
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}[Converse fundamental theorem for norm-$C^1$ paths]
Let $a<b$ be real numbers, and let $E$ be a compatible real-complete
ring-valued normed module over $R=\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$.  Let
$U:[a,b]\to E$ be norm-continuous and norm-differentiable on $(a,b)$, with
norm-continuous derivative $U':[a,b]\to E$.  Assume the interval integrals of
$U'$ exist.  Then, for all $\alpha,\beta\in[a,b]$,
\[
  U(\beta)-U(\alpha)=\int_\alpha^\beta U'(t)\,dt .
\]
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to prove the claim for $\alpha<\beta$.  Put
\[
  V(y)=U(y)-U(\alpha)-\int_\alpha^y U'(t)\,dt .
\]
The first fundamental-theorem statement gives
$\left(\int_\alpha^y U'(t)\,dt\right)'=U'(y)$ for $y\in(\alpha,\beta)$.
Hence $V'(y)=0$ on $(\alpha,\beta)$.  The preceding lemma gives
$V(\beta)=V(\alpha)=0$, which is the desired identity.  The oriented case
$\beta<\alpha$ follows by changing the sign of the interval integral.
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}[Leibniz rule for a moving upper limit]
Let $T>0$, let $E$ be a compatible real-complete ring-valued normed module
over $R=\SUPER(\mathbb{R})$, and let $q:[0,T]\times[0,T]\to E$ be
norm-continuous.  Suppose that the partial derivative $\partial_2 q$ exists
with respect to the second variable and is norm-continuous, and assume that
all displayed interval integrals exist.  For
\[
  H(t)\eqdef\int_0^t q(s,t)\,ds
\]
and $t\in(0,T)$,
\[
  H'(t)=q(t,t)+\int_0^t \partial_2q(s,t)\,ds .
\]
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
For $h\neq0$ sufficiently small,
\[
  \frac{H(t+h)-H(t)}{h}
  =
  \int_0^t\frac{q(s,t+h)-q(s,t)}{h}\,ds
  +\frac{1}{h}\int_t^{t+h}q(s,t+h)\,ds .
\]
By the converse fundamental theorem applied in the second variable,
\[
  \frac{q(s,t+h)-q(s,t)}{h}-\partial_2q(s,t)
  =
  \frac{1}{h}\int_t^{t+h}
  \bigl(\partial_2q(s,\tau)-\partial_2q(s,t)\bigr)\,d\tau .
\]
Uniform norm-continuity of $\partial_2q$ on the compact rectangle implies
that the right-hand side converges uniformly to $0$ for
$s\in[0,t]$.  The bounded-integral estimate then gives
\[
  \int_0^t\frac{q(s,t+h)-q(s,t)}{h}\,ds
  \longrightarrow
  \int_0^t\partial_2q(s,t)\,ds
\]
in the norm topology.

For the endpoint term,
\[
  \frac{1}{h}\int_t^{t+h}q(s,t+h)\,ds-q(t,t)
  =
  \frac{1}{h}\int_t^{t+h}\bigl(q(s,t+h)-q(t,t)\bigr)\,ds .
\]
Norm-continuity of $q$ at $(t,t)$ and the bounded-integral estimate show that
this tends to $0$.  Combining the two limits gives the formula.
\end{proof}

\section{Inhomogenious linear differential equations}

Theorem elaborated from~\cite{duhamel-chat}.

\begin{defn}
\emph{Inhomogeneous linear evolution} with~$\Delta$ as the differential operator is
\begin{equation*}
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
(\partial_t - \Delta)u &= f;\\
u({\cdot},0) &= 0.
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{equation*}
\end{defn}

\begin{thm}[First order Duhamel's principle]
Let $T>0$, and let $E$ be a compatible real-complete ring-valued normed
module of supersingular functions on which $\Delta:E\to E$ is a continuous
$R$-linear map.  For $s\in[0,T]$, define auxiliary equations
\begin{equation*}
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
(\partial_t - \Delta)u_s(t) &= 0;\\
u_s(s) &= f(s).
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{equation*}
Assume these equations have solutions $u_s(t)\in E$ such that
$q(s,t)\eqdef u_s(t)$ and
$\partial_2q(s,t)=\partial_tu_s(t)$ are norm-continuous on $[0,T]^2$, and
assume all displayed interval integrals exist.  Then
\[
  u(t) = \int_0^t u_s(t)\,ds
\]
satisfies the inhomogeneous linear evolution equation in the norm topology, and
hence also in the supersingular-funcoid topology.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
The integral defining $u$ exists by hypothesis, and $u(0)=0$ is immediate.

By the Leibniz rule for an integral with moving upper limit,
\[
  \partial_t u(t)
  =
  \partial_t \int_0^t u_s(t)\,ds
  =
  u_t(t)+\int_0^t \partial_tu_s(t)\,ds .
\]
Here $u_t(t)$ is the member of the family with parameter $s=t$, evaluated at
time~$t$, so $u_t(t)=f(t)$ by the auxiliary initial condition.
Since $\Delta:E\to E$ is continuous and $R$-linear, the continuous-linear-map
property for integrals gives
\[
  \Delta u(t)=\int_0^t \Delta u_s(t)\,ds .
\]
\begin{multline*}
(\partial_t - \Delta)u(t)
= u_t(t)+\int_0^t \bigl(\partial_tu_s(t)-\Delta u_s(t)\bigr)\,ds \\
= f(t),
\end{multline*}
so $(\partial_t - \Delta)u = f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}[Semigroup form of Duhamel's principle]\label{thm:semigroup-duhamel}
Let $T>0$, let $E$ be a compatible real-complete ring-valued normed module, and
let $A:E\to E$ be a continuous $R$-linear map.  Suppose that
$G_r:E\to E$, $0\leq r\leq T$, is a family of continuous $R$-linear maps such
that $G_0=I$, $G_{r+\rho}=G_rG_\rho$ whenever $r,\rho,r+\rho\in[0,T]$, and
the map $(r,x)\mapsto G_rx$ is norm-continuous.  Suppose also that, for every
$x\in E$, the path $r\mapsto G_rx$ is norm-$C^1$ and
\[
  \frac{d}{dr}G_rx=AG_rx=G_rAx .
\]
Assume moreover that $(r,x)\mapsto AG_rx$ is norm-continuous on
$[0,T]\times E$.
Let $F:[0,T]\to E$ be norm-continuous, and assume that all displayed interval
integrals exist.  Then
\[
  u(t)\eqdef G_tu_0+\int_0^tG_{t-s}F(s)\,ds
\]
is norm-$C^1$ on $(0,T)$ and satisfies
\[
  u'(t)-Au(t)=F(t),\qquad u(0)=u_0 .
\]
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
Put $H(t)=\int_0^tG_{t-s}F(s)\,ds$.  For $h>0$ small,
\begin{multline*}
  \frac{H(t+h)-H(t)}{h}
  =
  \int_0^t
  \frac{G_{t+h-s}F(s)-G_{t-s}F(s)}{h}\,ds\\
  +\frac1h\int_t^{t+h}G_{t+h-s}F(s)\,ds .
\end{multline*}
By the assumed norm-$C^1$ dependence of $G_r$,
\[
  \frac{G_{t+h-s}F(s)-G_{t-s}F(s)}{h}
  \longrightarrow AG_{t-s}F(s)
\]
uniformly for $s\in[0,t]$ on compact subintervals on which the stated
continuities hold; the bounded-integral estimate passes this limit through the
integral.  The second term converges to $F(t)$ by norm-continuity of the
semigroup action, $G_0=I$, and norm-continuity of $F$ at~$t$.  Hence the
right derivative of $H$ equals
\[
  F(t)+\int_0^tAG_{t-s}F(s)\,ds .
\]
The same computation for $h<0$ uses
\[
H(t+h)-H(t)
=\int_0^{t+h}(G_{t+h-s}-G_{t-s})F(s)\,ds
-\int_{t+h}^tG_{t-s}F(s)\,ds ,
\]
and gives the same left derivative.  Therefore
\[
  H'(t)=F(t)+\int_0^tAG_{t-s}F(s)\,ds .
\]
Since $A$ is continuous and $R$-linear, it passes through the integral:
\[
  A\int_0^tG_{t-s}F(s)\,ds
  =\int_0^tAG_{t-s}F(s)\,ds .
\]
Also $\frac{d}{dt}G_tu_0=AG_tu_0$.  Combining these identities gives
$u'(t)-Au(t)=F(t)$, and $u(0)=G_0u_0=u_0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Rewriting in integral form}

Let $\mathbb{P}$ denote the Leray projection.
Write $G_t=e^{\nu t\Delta}$.
The point of the next definition is to isolate exactly the operator-theoretic
hypotheses needed to transport the classical Banach-space Duhamel argument to
the $\SUPER$/$\fSNG$ setting.

\begin{defn}[Admissible projected-heat setting]\label{def:admissible-projected-heat}
Fix $T>0$ and data $u_0,f$.  An admissible projected-heat setting on
$[0,T]$ consists of a compatible real-complete $R$-valued normed module $E$ of
generalized vector fields, a path class
$\mathcal X_T\subseteq C([0,T];E)$, and operators $\mathbb{P}$ and
$G_t$, $0\leq t\leq T$, satisfying the following assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G_t$ satisfies the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:semigroup-duhamel} with $A=\nu\Delta$.
\item $\mathbb{P}:E\to E$ is a continuous $R$-linear projection.  On the
objects on which the displayed expressions are used, it annihilates gradients,
is the identity on divergence-free vector fields, and commutes with $\Delta$
and $G_t$.
\item For each $v\in\mathcal X_T$, the tensor product $v\otimes v$,
$\nabla\cdot(v\otimes v)$, and
\[
  \mathcal N(v)(t)\eqdef
  \mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(v(t)\otimes v(t))
\]
are defined in $E$, and $\mathcal N(v):[0,T]\to E$ is norm-continuous.
Also $\mathbb{P}f:[0,T]\to E$ is norm-continuous.
\item For every $v\in\mathcal X_T$ and $0\leq t\leq T$, the integrals
\[
  \int_0^tG_{t-s}\mathcal N(v)(s)\,ds,
  \qquad
  \int_0^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)\,ds
\]
exist absolutely in $E$.
\end{enumerate}
These are hypotheses, not consequences of smoothness alone.  In particular,
on $\mathbb{R}^N$ a $C^\infty$ function need not be in $L^1$.
\end{defn}

\begin{lem}\label{lem:admissible-integrand}
In an admissible projected-heat setting, if $u\in\mathcal X_T$, then for each
$0\leq t\leq T$ the functions
\[
  s\mapsto G_{t-s}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u(s)\otimes u(s)),
  \qquad
  s\mapsto G_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)
\]
are the admissible integrands in the mild formula, and their interval
integrals exist in $E$.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
This is exactly the mapping and integrability content of
Definition~\ref{def:admissible-projected-heat}.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:super-does-not-prove-admissibility}
The admissibility assumptions in
Definition~\ref{def:admissible-projected-heat} do not follow from the
properties of $\SUPER$ established in~\cite[theorem~6, p.~10]{limit} and from
the linearity of generalized derivatives and integrals stated
in~\cite[p.~10]{limit}.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The cited limit-extension theorem quantifies over continuous finitary
multiargument functions on the base value space and preserves algebraic
identities formed from those operations.  The cited application paragraph gives
linearity of generalized derivatives and definite integrals.  These are
pointwise, finite-arity, or linearity facts; they do not state mapping theorems
for arbitrary nonlocal operators on function spaces.

By contrast, the heat semigroup and the Leray projection are nonlocal
operators.  Classically,
\[
  G_t v(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^N}K_t(x-y)v(y)\,dy
\]
and $\mathbb{P}$ is a Fourier multiplier, equivalently a singular-integral
operator.  Their values at a point depend on the behavior of the whole vector
field, not only on finitely many pointwise values.  Therefore their definition,
continuity on a chosen normed module, commutation with $\Delta$, and
compatibility with the generalized limit construction require operator
theorems in that module.  The finitary identity theorem does not by itself give
convolution identities, Fourier inversion, Fubini/Tonelli rules, or
singular-integral boundedness.

The same applies to the Duhamel integrals.  The integral used in this paper is
defined only when the relevant simple-function approximations are absolutely
convergent in a specified compatible real-complete module.  The $\SUPER$
construction and the linearity of generalized integrals do not give the real
norm bounds, completeness, or absolute-convergence estimates needed for those
integrals.  Hence a full discharge of
Definition~\ref{def:admissible-projected-heat} would require additional
analytic theorems specifying the module and proving these mapping and
convergence properties.
\end{proof}

\begin{defn}
In an admissible projected-heat setting, I call $u\in\mathcal X_T$ a
supersingular mild solution of the Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes equations when
\[
u(t) = G_t u_0 - \int_0^t G_{t-s} \mathbb{P} \nabla \cdot (u \otimes u)(s) ds
+ \int_0^t G_{t-s} \mathbb{P} f(s) ds
\]
for all $0\leq t\leq T$.\footnote{This formula is usually called the
\emph{mild (Duhamel) formulation}.}
\end{defn}

\begin{thm}[Projected mild equation in an admissible setting]\label{thm:projected-mild}
In an admissible projected-heat setting, let
\[
  F_u(t)\eqdef -\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)(t)+\mathbb{P}f(t).
\]
If $u\in\mathcal X_T$ is norm-$C^1$ on $(0,T)$ and
\[
  u(t)=G_tu_0+\int_0^tG_{t-s}F_u(s)\,ds,
\]
then
\[
  \partial_tu(t)-\nu\Delta u(t)=F_u(t),
\]
hence
\[
  \partial_tu-\nu\Delta u+\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=\mathbb{P}f.
\]
This is the direct $\SUPER$/$\fSNG$ analogue of the classical Banach-space
Duhamel step: the semigroup, projection, and Duhamel-integrability hypotheses
are made explicit in Definition~\ref{def:admissible-projected-heat}.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:semigroup-duhamel} with $A=\nu\Delta$ and
$F=F_u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{defn}[Admissible pressure recovery]\label{def:pressure-recovery}
Let $u$ be a solution of the projected equation in an admissible
projected-heat setting, and put
\[
  h_u(t)\eqdef f(t)-\nabla\cdot(u(t)\otimes u(t)).
\]
Pressure recovery for $u$ is called admissible on $[0,T]$ if the following
additional facts have been proved in the same function-space framework:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $u$ is divergence-free and
\[
  \nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=(u\cdot\nabla)u .
\]
\item The complementary part $(I-\mathbb{P})h_u(t)$ is defined for every
$t\in[0,T]$ and belongs to the range of the gradient operator.
\item There is a scalar function $p$ with the required space and time
regularity, unique up to addition of a function of $t$, such that
\[
  \nabla p(t)=(I-\mathbb{P})h_u(t).
\]
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, $p$ must solve the compatible Helmholtz/Poisson reconstruction
problem associated with the chosen spaces.  Formally it satisfies
\[
  \Delta p(t)=\nabla\cdot f(t)
  -\nabla\cdot\nabla\cdot(u(t)\otimes u(t)),
\]
but this scalar Poisson equation alone is not sufficient unless the function
spaces, boundary or decay conditions, and Helmholtz decomposition have already
been specified.
\end{defn}

\begin{prop}[Sobolev Helmholtz/Poisson reconstruction]\label{prop:sobolev-pressure-recovery}
Let $s\in\mathbb{R}$.  On $H^s(\mathbb{R}^N)^N$ define the classical Leray
projection by the Fourier multiplier
\[
  \widehat{(\mathbb{P}h)}_i(\xi)
  =
  \sum_{j=1}^N
  \left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{\xi_i\xi_j}{\abs{\xi}^2}\right)\widehat h_j(\xi),
  \qquad \xi\neq0,
\]
with any value assigned at $\xi=0$.  Then for every
$h\in H^s(\mathbb{R}^N)^N$ there is a tempered distribution
$p\in\mathcal S'(\mathbb{R}^N)$, unique up to addition of a constant, such
that
\[
  \nabla p=(I-\mathbb{P})h,
  \qquad
  \Delta p=\nabla\cdot h
\]
in the sense of distributions.  If
\[
  h\in C^k([0,T];H^s(\mathbb{R}^N)^N),
\]
then
\[
  \nabla p\in C^k([0,T];H^s(\mathbb{R}^N)^N).
\]

Consequently, if $m>N/2+1$, $u\in C^k([0,T];H^m(\mathbb{R}^N)^N)$ is
divergence-free, and $f\in C^k([0,T];H^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^N)^N)$, then
pressure recovery for $u$ is admissible with
$h_u=f-\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)$ and $s=m-1$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The multiplier matrices defining $\mathbb{P}$ and $I-\mathbb{P}$ are bounded
orthogonal projections for each $\xi\neq0$, so they define bounded operators
on $H^s(\mathbb{R}^N)^N$.  Moreover
\[
  \xi\cdot\widehat{\mathbb{P}h}(\xi)=0
\]
for $\xi\neq0$, hence $\mathbb{P}h$ is divergence-free in the distributional
sense.  The complementary field
\[
  (I-\mathbb{P})h
\]
has Fourier transform parallel to $\xi$, so its distributional curl is zero.
The Poincare lemma for distributions on $\mathbb{R}^N$ therefore gives
$p\in\mathcal S'(\mathbb{R}^N)$, unique up to a constant, with
\[
  \nabla p=(I-\mathbb{P})h .
\]
Taking divergence gives
\[
  \Delta p=\nabla\cdot(I-\mathbb{P})h
  =\nabla\cdot h-\nabla\cdot\mathbb{P}h
  =\nabla\cdot h .
\]
If $h$ depends $C^k$ on time with values in $H^s$, then
$\nabla p=(I-\mathbb{P})h$ has the same time regularity because
$I-\mathbb{P}$ is bounded on $H^s$.

For the final assertion, $H^m(\mathbb{R}^N)$ is an algebra when $m>N/2$, so
$u\otimes u\in C^k([0,T];H^m)$ and
$\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)\in C^k([0,T];H^{m-1})$.  Since $u$ is
divergence-free,
\[
  \nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=(u\cdot\nabla)u .
\]
Applying the first part to
$h_u=f-\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)\in C^k([0,T];H^{m-1})^N$ verifies the
gradient and Poisson reconstruction requirements in
Definition~\ref{def:pressure-recovery}.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}[From projected equation to pressure form]\label{prop:pressure-recovery}
Assume that $u$ satisfies the projected equation
\[
  \partial_tu-\nu\Delta u+\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=\mathbb{P}f
\]
on $[0,T]$, and that pressure recovery for $u$ is admissible in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:pressure-recovery}.  Then $(u,p)$ satisfies
\[
  \partial_tu-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=f
\]
on $[0,T]$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The projected equation is
\[
  \partial_tu-\nu\Delta u=\mathbb{P}h_u .
\]
By admissible pressure recovery,
\[
  h_u=\mathbb{P}h_u+(I-\mathbb{P})h_u
      =\mathbb{P}h_u+\nabla p .
\]
Therefore
\[
  \partial_tu-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p=h_u
  =f-\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u).
\]
Since $u$ is divergence-free, $\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=(u\cdot\nabla)u$,
which gives the asserted pressure form.
\end{proof}

\section{The sequence of approximate solutions}

In an admissible projected-heat setting, define the Picard iteration
mapping~$\mathcal{T}$ on paths $v\in\mathcal X_T$ for which the right-hand side
belongs to $\mathcal X_T$ by
\begin{equation*}
(\mathcal{T}v)(t) = G_t u_0 - \int_0^t G_{t-s}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(v\otimes v)(s)ds + \int_0^t G_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)ds.
\end{equation*}
So, we have a sequence
\[ u^{(0)}(t) = u_0,\quad u^{(n+1)}(t) = (\mathcal{T}u^{(n)})(t). \]

The needed integrability of the two Duhamel terms is part of
Definition~\ref{def:admissible-projected-heat}; it is not implied merely by
$C^\infty$ regularity on $\mathbb{R}^N$.

The following formal variant proves the weaker unrestricted-in-time statement
obtained by allowing values in $\SUPER$ or $\fSNG$ instead of requiring
ordinary real-valued vector fields at every step.  After these iterates are
formed, one can still try as the solution
$u(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n)}(t)$, the generalized limit of this sequence.

\begin{defn}[Formal supersingular Picard operations]\label{def:formal-super-picard}
For a path $q:[0,\infty)\to\fSNG({-},R^N)$ define the formal supersingular
time integral by
\[
  \mathcal I_t^{\SUPER}(q)
  \eqdef
  \xlim_{k\to\infty}
  \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{t}{k}\,q\left(\frac{jt}{k}\right),
  \qquad t\geq0.
\]
The generalized limit exists for every sequence, so this expression is always
defined as a $\SUPER$-valued vector field.  When the ordinary Banach-valued or
Lebesgue integral exists, $\mathcal I_t^{\SUPER}(q)$ agrees with it by agreement
of $\xlim$ with ordinary limits and by linearity.

In the same formal supersingular sense, the nonlocal operators $G_t$ and
$\mathbb{P}$ are understood as their regularized and truncated classical
formulas followed by $\xlim$ over the regularization and truncation
parameters.  Thus they are total operations on the corresponding
$\SUPER$-valued vector fields, possibly after raising the singularity level.
This is a weaker assertion than boundedness on a classical Banach space.
\end{defn}

\begin{thm}[Unrestricted supersingular Picard iterates]
\label{thm:unrestricted-super-picard}
Interpret the Picard formula with the formal supersingular operations of
Definition~\ref{def:formal-super-picard}.  If $u_0$ and $f$ are embedded in
the supersingular vector-field universe, then for every $n\in\mathbb N$ and
every finite $t\geq0$, the Picard iterate $u^{(n)}(t)$ is defined as a
$\SUPER$-valued vector field.  If the chosen representatives of the formal
operations land in the closed $\fSNG$ part, then the iterates are
$\fSNG$-valued; otherwise they are still $\SUPER$-valued after finitely many
level raises.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
The proof is by induction on $n$.  The initial path
$u^{(0)}(t)=u_0$ is defined for all $t\geq0$ by embedding $u_0$ into the
supersingular universe.

Assume $u^{(n)}(t)$ is defined for every finite $t\geq0$.  The tensor product
$u^{(n)}\otimes u^{(n)}$ is obtained from pointwise multiplication and
addition of coordinates, hence is defined by the extension of continuous
finitary operations.  The divergence
\[
  \nabla\cdot(u^{(n)}\otimes u^{(n)})
\]
is defined because $\fSNG$ is closed under generalized differential
operators, again with possible level raising in $\SUPER$.  By
Definition~\ref{def:formal-super-picard}, applying $\mathbb{P}$ and then
$G_{t-s}$ to this vector field is defined for every $0\leq s\leq t$, and the
same is true for $G_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)$.

The two Duhamel terms are then
\[
  \mathcal I_t^{\SUPER}
  \bigl(s\mapsto
  G_{t-s}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u^{(n)}\otimes u^{(n)})(s)\bigr)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \mathcal I_t^{\SUPER}
  \bigl(s\mapsto G_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)\bigr),
\]
so they are defined for every finite $t\geq0$ by the definition of
$\mathcal I_t^{\SUPER}$.  Finally, addition and subtraction are continuous
finitary operations and therefore have supersingular extensions.  Hence
\[
  u^{(n+1)}(t)
  =
  G_tu_0
  -\mathcal I_t^{\SUPER}
  \bigl(s\mapsto
  G_{t-s}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u^{(n)}\otimes u^{(n)})(s)\bigr)
  +\mathcal I_t^{\SUPER}
  \bigl(s\mapsto G_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)\bigr)
\]
is defined for every finite $t\geq0$.  No estimate in the induction depends on
an upper endpoint $T$, so the construction is unrestricted in time.  The
last assertion follows from whether the selected formal extensions stay in
$\fSNG$ or only in the larger hierarchy $\SUPER$.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:unrestricted-super-picard} proves only that each finite
Picard iterate can be formed for all times after allowing supersingular
values.  It does not prove convergence of the Picard sequence in a classical
space, boundedness of the generalized operators, or that the eventual
generalized-limit solution is $\mathbb{R}^N$-valued.
\end{rem}

\begin{prop}[Shift invariance for a convergent Picard sequence]\label{prop:picard-shift}
Let $E$ be a compatible real-complete ring-valued normed module and let
$(x_n)$ be an $E$-valued sequence converging in the norm topology to $x$.
Assume that the extended limit $\xlim$ agrees with the ordinary limit on
convergent $E$-valued sequences and is linear.  Then
\[
  \xlim_{n\to\infty}x_{n+1}=\xlim_{n\to\infty}x_n .
\]
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Since $x_n\to x$, also $x_{n+1}\to x$.  Hence the defining agreement with
ordinary limits gives
\[
  \xlim_{n\to\infty}x_n=x
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \xlim_{n\to\infty}x_{n+1}=x.
\]
Equivalently, $x_{n+1}-x_n\to0$, so linearity gives
\[
  \xlim_{n\to\infty}x_{n+1}-\xlim_{n\to\infty}x_n
  =
  \xlim_{n\to\infty}(x_{n+1}-x_n)=0.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}[Local Sobolev Picard admissibility and convergence]
\label{thm:local-sobolev-picard}
Let $m>N/2+1$, let
$u_0\in H^m(\mathbb{R}^N)^N$ be divergence-free, and let
\[
  f\in C([0,T_0];H^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^N)^N).
\]
Then there is $0<T\leq T_0$ and $R>0$ such that the Picard map
$\mathcal T$ maps the closed ball
\[
  B_R\eqdef
  \setcond{v\in C([0,T];H^m(\mathbb{R}^N)^N)}
  {\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\norm{v(t)}_{H^m}\leq R}
\]
to itself, all Duhamel integrals displayed in the Picard formula exist as
absolutely convergent $H^m$-valued Bochner integrals, and
$\mathcal T$ is a contraction on $B_R$.  Consequently the Picard iterates stay
in $B_R$ and converge in $C([0,T];H^m)$ to the unique mild solution on
$[0,T]$.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
We use the standard Sobolev estimates on $\mathbb{R}^N$.  Since
$m>N/2$, $H^m$ is an algebra.  Since the heat semigroup gains one derivative,
there is a constant $C_m$ such that, for $0<r\leq T_0$,
\[
  \norm{G_r\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(a\otimes b)}_{H^m}
  \leq C_m r^{-1/2}\norm{a}_{H^m}\norm{b}_{H^m},
\]
and
\[
  \norm{G_r\mathbb{P}g}_{H^m}
  \leq C_m r^{-1/2}\norm{g}_{H^{m-1}} .
\]
The value at $r=0$ is irrelevant for these Bochner integrals, since it occurs
only on a set of measure zero.

Put
\[
  A=\norm{u_0}_{H^m},\qquad
  F=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_0}\norm{\mathbb{P}f(t)}_{H^{m-1}},
\]
and choose $R\geq 2A+1$.  For $v\in B_R$,
\[
  \norm{(\mathcal Tv)(t)}_{H^m}
  \leq
  A+C_m\int_0^t(t-s)^{-1/2}
  \bigl(\norm{v(s)}_{H^m}^2+\norm{\mathbb{P}f(s)}_{H^{m-1}}\bigr)\,ds .
\]
Thus
\[
  \sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\norm{(\mathcal Tv)(t)}_{H^m}
  \leq A+2C_mT^{1/2}(R^2+F).
\]
Choose $T$ small enough that
\[
  2C_mT^{1/2}(R^2+F)\leq R-A .
\]
Then $\mathcal T(B_R)\subseteq B_R$.

The same estimate proves absolute convergence of the two Duhamel integrals:
their $H^m$ norms are dominated by an integrable multiple of
$(t-s)^{-1/2}$ on $[0,t]$.  Continuity in $t$ follows from the strong
continuity of $G_r$ and dominated convergence, using the same singular
majorant.

For $v,w\in B_R$,
\[
  v\otimes v-w\otimes w=(v-w)\otimes v+w\otimes(v-w),
\]
so the Sobolev algebra estimate gives
\[
  \norm{v(s)\otimes v(s)-w(s)\otimes w(s)}_{H^m}
  \leq C_m R\norm{v(s)-w(s)}_{H^m},
\]
after increasing $C_m$ if necessary.  Therefore
\[
  \sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\norm{\mathcal Tv(t)-\mathcal Tw(t)}_{H^m}
  \leq
  2C_mRT^{1/2}
  \sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\norm{v(t)-w(t)}_{H^m}.
\]
Shrinking $T$ further so that $2C_mRT^{1/2}<1$, $\mathcal T$ is a contraction.
Banach's fixed point theorem gives convergence of the Picard iterates in
$C([0,T];H^m)$, and the fixed point satisfies the mild equation.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-sobolev-picard} proves the hard admissibility point
locally in a classical space: the iterates remain in a space where the
displayed Duhamel integrals are meaningful, and the Picard sequence actually
converges in $H^m$, hence also after embedding into the supersingular
framework.  This is still not a global proof on $\mathbb{R}^N$ for arbitrary
smooth data without Sobolev bounds; extending the estimates uniformly in time
is the unresolved regularity problem.
\end{rem}

\section{Interchange of limit and integration}

In the local Sobolev setting of
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-sobolev-picard}, the passage to the limit in the
Picard formula is justified by ordinary Banach-space convergence.  Namely,
$u^{(n)}\to u$ in $C([0,T];H^m)$ and
Proposition~\ref{prop:picard-shift} gives
\[
  \xlim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n+1)}=\xlim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n)}=u.
\]
The estimates used in Theorem~\ref{thm:local-sobolev-picard} also give an
integrable majorant for the nonlinear Duhamel terms.  Therefore dominated
convergence in $H^m$ gives

\begin{multline*}
u(t) = \lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n+1)}(t) \\
= G_t u_0 - \int_0^t \lim_{n\to\infty}
\bigl(G_{t-s} \mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u^{(n)}\otimes u^{(n)})(s)\bigr)ds
+ \int_0^t G_{t-s} \mathbb{P}f(s)ds \\
= G_t u_0 - \int_0^t G_{t-s} \mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)(s)ds
+ \int_0^t G_{t-s} \mathbb{P}f(s)ds,
\end{multline*}
that is it is a solution.

Within such a Banach/Sobolev admissible setting, boundedness follows from the
generalized limit theorem in~\cite{limit}: continuous finitary operations on
the target commute with generalized limits. In particular, applying it to the
continuous unary operation $v\mapsto \norm{v}$ gives
\[
  \norm{\xlim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n)}} = \xlim_{n\to\infty}\norm{u^{(n)}} \leq
  \sup_n \norm{u^{(n)}}.
\]
So the generalized limit is bounded on the Banach/Sobolev data used here.
No separate theorem interchanging generalized limits with arbitrary
supersingular integrals is needed for this Sobolev step, because the displayed
Duhamel integrals are ordinary $H^m$-valued Bochner integrals and the passage
to the limit is the classical dominated-convergence argument above.

\subsection{Endpoint criterion}

The standard way to prove that a
candidate solution is classical at a possible critical time is instead a
continuation criterion in a classical Banach space.

Let $X$ be a classical solution space for which the local Na\-vi\-er-Sto\-kes
theory is available, for instance $H^m(\mathbb{R}^N)$ with
$m>N/2+1$ and divergence-free data.  Suppose that the generalized mild solution
$u$ agrees with a classical solution on $[0,T)$ and that, for some real
number $M$,
\begin{equation}\label{endpoint-bound}
  \sup_{0<t<T}\norm{u(t)}_X \leq M < \infty .
\end{equation}
Then $u(t)$ has a limit in $X$ as $t\uparrow T$ provided the mild formula gives
the usual time-continuity estimates in $X$.  More explicitly, for
$0<r<t<T$,
\begin{multline*}
u(t)-u(r)
= (G_{t-r}-I)u(r) \\
-\int_r^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)(s)\,ds
-\int_0^r(G_{t-s}-G_{r-s})\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)(s)\,ds \\
{}+\int_r^tG_{t-s}\mathbb{P}f(s)\,ds
{}+\int_0^r(G_{t-s}-G_{r-s})\mathbb{P}f(s)\,ds .
\end{multline*}
If the terms on the right tend to zero in $X$, uniformly for $r,t\uparrow T$,
then $u(t)$ is Cauchy in $X$.  Since $X$ is complete, there is
$u_T\in X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^N$ with $u(t)\to u_T$ in $X$.  Applying the local
existence theorem with initial data $u_T$ continues the classical solution
beyond $T$.

Thus, a proof of classicality at $t_{\operatorname{crit}}$ would
follow from a classical a priori bound such as~\eqref{endpoint-bound}, together
with the corresponding time-continuity estimates for the nonlinear Duhamel
term.  The present generalized-limit construction does not yet provide this
bound.  Therefore the claim that $u(t_{\operatorname{crit}})$ is classical is
not proved by the current argument.

\begin{rem}
Using the ring-valued Sobolev quantity above, a sufficient form of
the missing bound would be
\begin{equation}\label{super-endpoint-bound}
  \norm{u(t)}_{H^m_{\SUPER}}\leq M
  \quad\text{for all }0<t<T
\end{equation}
with one real number $M$.  Since $u(t)$ is classical on $[0,T)$, the
ring-valued norm agrees there with the ordinary $H^m$ norm, and
\eqref{super-endpoint-bound} implies~\eqref{endpoint-bound}.
\end{rem}

\begin{rem}
The estimate~\eqref{super-endpoint-bound} cannot be obtained merely by
repeating the classical local theory in $\SUPER$ or $\fSNG$.  For
$X=H^m(\mathbb{R}^N)$, $m>N/2+1$, the classical calculation gives a differential
inequality of the form
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}\norm{u(t)}_{H^m}
  \leq C\norm{u(t)}_{H^m}^2+\norm{f(t)}_{H^m},
\]
which gives a bound only on a short time interval depending on the initial
norm.  Iterating this local estimate up to a prescribed time $T$ requires an
independent a priori bound preventing the $H^m$ norm from blowing up before
$T$.  In dimension three, proving such a bound for arbitrary smooth data is
precisely the unresolved regularity problem.

The ring-valued version of the same computation would at best give a
ring-valued differential inequality, assuming all products, integrals, and
suprema involved are defined.  To use it for classical continuation one still
needs a comparison theorem showing that the ring-valued inequality implies a
real bound~\eqref{super-endpoint-bound}.  One also needs a theorem that the
generalized limit of the Picard iterates preserves uniform real $H^m$ bounds:
\[
  \sup_n\norm{u^{(n)}(t)}_{H^m}\leq M
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \norm{\xlim_{n\to\infty}u^{(n)}(t)}_{H^m_{\SUPER}}\leq M .
\]
Without such comparison and boundedness theorems, the ring-valued norms do not
justify~\eqref{endpoint-bound}.
\end{rem}

For further contradiction to come, one would need to prove such an estimate and
then assume that such $t_{\operatorname{crit}}$ exists.
